Fast.com: Netflix's ad-free internet speed test

By AiCoN | Published 19 October 2026 | Sources checked 19 October 2026

Fast.com is a browser speed test run by Netflix. Its page calls it an ad-free way to estimate the speed your internet provider gives you.

What it is

Fast.com is a website that tests your internet speed. Open it in a browser and the test starts. Netflix runs it. The page says: "FAST.com gives you an estimate of your current Internet speed." It is not an AI tool.

Is it free?

No page we read says "free" or shows a price, and we saw no sign-up. What the page does say: Netflix offers it as "a simple, quick, ad-free way" to estimate the speed your ISP (internet provider) gives you. Netflix's own engineering blog says it is for any internet user "whether they are a Netflix member or not". Fast.com's page says it tests "globally on any device (phone, laptop, or smart TV with browser)".

What it shows, per Fast.com

  • Download speed first. The page says download speed matters most for people who consume content online.
  • More info on request: click "Show more info" to see upload speed and latency (ping), including "unloaded" and "loaded" latency. The page says the gap between them is also called "bufferbloat".
  • How: it runs a series of downloads from and uploads to Netflix servers and reports the maximum speed your connection can give.
  • Not for enterprise use: the page says it is built for individuals and is not supported for third-party service certification.

How to use it

  1. Open fast.com in any browser.
  2. Wait for the number to settle. Click "Show more info" if you want upload and ping.
  3. If results often fall short of what you pay for, the page suggests asking your ISP about them.

For a sense of what you need for video, Netflix's help page recommends 3 Mbps or higher for HD (720p), 5 Mbps for full HD (1080p) and 15 Mbps for 4K.

What to watch out for

  • It is an estimate, in Fast.com's own word. Other tests and servers can show different numbers. Our advice: close other downloads first and test a few times.
  • Data use: a test downloads and uploads data. We did not see how much it uses, so on a limited mobile data plan, our advice is to check first or test on Wi-Fi.
  • It measures against Netflix servers. We did not test it.

Privacy and data

  • Netflix's Fast.com Privacy Statement says Netflix, Inc. is the data controller.
  • It says it collects automatically: your activity on Fast.com, device IDs, device and software details, connection information (speed, latency, wifi or cellular), your IP address, which "may tell us your general location and internet service provider", and browser and web server log information.
  • If you contact customer service or take part in surveys, it may also collect your name, email and phone number. Privacy questions: privacy@netflix.com.

India

  • The page says it works globally. We did not test it from India. We saw no price, so we quote no INR price.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fast.com free?

No page says "free" or lists a price. It describes itself as ad-free and needs no Netflix membership, per Netflix's blog.

Does it test upload speed?

Yes, under "Show more info", per the page.

Who runs it?

Netflix.
