SiMa.ai funding: Physical AI chips and the 2028 hardware plan

By AiCoN | Published 7 October 2026 | Sources checked 7 October 2026

SiMa.ai announced funding for chips and software used in physical machines. The round is confirmed in its release, but future hardware targets and speed claims are not products already delivered.

What funding was announced?

On 28 September 2026, SiMa.ai announced USD 150 million in Series C financing, bringing total capital raised to USD 500 million and valuing the company at USD 1.45 billion. Fidelity Management & Research Company and Amplify co-led the round.

The release lists existing participants and new investors, including AllianceBernstein, Baron Capital, J.P. Morgan and the State of Michigan. A valuation is a financing measure, not company revenue, cash paid to every shareholder or a guaranteed resale value.

At the approximate ₹96.43-per-dollar reference used in this archive, USD 150 million is about ₹1,446 crore. That comparison differs from the original ₹1,330 crore headline because it uses a different stated exchange-rate reference. The official funding amount is in US dollars.

What is Physical AI?

Physical AI means AI used in machines that sense or act in the world, such as robots, drones or vehicles. SiMa.ai provides purpose-built silicon and software for this kind of work. Processing can run on a device rather than depend on a remote cloud for every step.

This does not mean the entire application is automatically offline, safe or autonomous. A real product still needs sensors, software, integration, power, cooling and a way to handle failures. For a vehicle or robot, speed is only one part of the design.

What will the money support?

The company says the round will scale Palette Neat, its agentic software environment for Physical AI, and fund next-generation hardware. It targets 1,000 dense TOPS and names machine-learning IP, chiplets and systems-on-chip as planned offerings.

TOPS means trillions of operations per second, a throughput measure. The exact operation type and workload matter. A headline TOPS number alone does not show latency, power consumption, accuracy or performance in your application.

The hardware target is H1 2028

The official release says the next-generation offerings are slated for the first half of 2028. That is a future plan, not proof of a shipping 1,000-TOPS device in October 2026. A migration promise also needs checking against the actual product and software versions.

The release claims deployments can move from months to days or hours. That is the company's claim, not an independent benchmark or a guaranteed timetable for every customer. Application complexity, model changes, hardware qualification and safety testing can all affect deployment work.

Is there a free product or retail investment offer?

This funding announcement gives no consumer free tier, universal chip price or retail-investment application. A funding round is not permission for readers to deposit money with someone using the company name.

For an engineering purchase, ask about the current hardware, development kit, software licence, support, availability and total integration cost. Do not buy from a roadmap figure alone or assume the new round makes every developer tool free.

How to assess a Physical AI platform

  1. Define the actual model, sensors and operating conditions.
  2. Check supported model formats and software versions.
  3. Measure end-to-end latency, accuracy, memory and power on your workload.
  4. Test failure handling and updates.
  5. Confirm hardware availability, support and production lifecycle.
  6. Keep safety validation separate from a performance demonstration.

India relevance and market forecasts

The release names Indian partners and customers among its network, including L&T Technology Services and Virya Autonomous Technologies in Bangalore. That is not a specific Kerala job, course or device offer.

The release cites Counterpoint's projection of 145 million Physical AI device shipments by 2035. A market projection is an estimate, not a count already achieved. It does not guarantee demand or returns for a particular product or investment.
