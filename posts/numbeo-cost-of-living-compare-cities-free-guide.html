Numbeo: compare cost-of-living estimates between cities, free

By AiCoN | Published 22 October 2026 | Sources checked 22 October 2026

Type a city, see what groceries, rent, restaurants and transport cost there, then compare it with another supported city side by side. Free to browse, no account needed. Here is what the official pages confirm and what our hands-on test with Mumbai and Delhi found.

Quick facts

  • What it is: a free crowdsourced database of what life costs around the world: groceries, rent, restaurants, transport, salaries and more. As of 22 October 2026 the site shows 9,945,456 prices in 12,899 cities, entered by 889,414 contributors.
  • Cost: free to browse, no account needed. The site earns from on-page ads and a paid data API for businesses.
  • Made by: Numbeo doo, a private company headquartered in Belgrade, Serbia. Founder and CEO is Mladen Adamovic, a former Google software engineer. Online since April 2009.
  • Works on: web browsers. Nothing to install. (We tested on desktop.)
  • Sections: Cost of Living, Property Prices, Quality of Life (crime, healthcare, pollution, traffic and more) and Compensation.
  • Link: numbeo.com

Why it is useful

Everyone wonders at some point: is rent really that much higher in Mumbai than Delhi? What would my salary need to be if I moved to another city? Numbeo answers with estimates built from what hundreds of thousands of people report paying, combined with automated market-data collection and statistical validation, per the site's own description.

Our hands-on test (22 October 2026)

We used numbeo.com in a desktop browser, with no account:

  • We typed "Mumbai" into the city search on the homepage. Autocomplete offered "Mumbai, India" and "Navi Mumbai, India"; one click took us to the Mumbai page.
  • The Mumbai page showed estimates in rupees: monthly costs of ₹135,318.0 for a family of four and ₹37,746.7 for a single person, both excluding rent. Below that, full price tables with ranges, for example a meal at an inexpensive restaurant at ₹500 (range ₹280-1,000) and a litre of milk at ₹69.23.
  • We then ran the comparison tool for Mumbai vs Delhi. It reported that the cost of living in Delhi is 14.7% lower than in Mumbai excluding rent, that rent in Delhi is 56.8% lower, and that you would need around ₹168,078 in Delhi to maintain the same standard of life that ₹240,000 affords in Mumbai, assuming you rent in both cities.
  • A currency selector offers around 150 currencies with a "sticky" option, and there is a switch between metric and US measurement units.

All of this worked without signing up for anything.

What you can use it for

  • Checking estimated living costs in a city before you move there for work or study.
  • Comparing two cities side by side, including the salary you would need to keep your standard of living.
  • Budgeting for a trip using local restaurant and transport prices.
  • Looking beyond prices: crime, healthcare, pollution and traffic indexes per city, plus property prices.
  • Contributing prices from your own city if you want to improve the data (an optional form, no account required).

Limits to know

  • The numbers are crowdsourced estimates. Item prices come with min-max ranges, while the summary indices do not, and accuracy depends on how many people have contributed recently in that city. Treat them as informed estimates, not official statistics.
  • The site carries advertising. We saw Google AdSense slots on the pages we tested.
  • The paid product is a data API aimed at companies. Browsing the website is the free part, and nothing we tested asked for payment.
  • We verified the search, city page and comparison tool described above in one desktop session. We did not test every city or the mobile layout.

Privacy

Browsing the database needs no account. Numbeo's privacy policy says personal details such as name and email are collected only when you volunteer them through forms, that its cookies contain no personally identifiable information, that server logs (IP address, browser type) are not linked to personally identifiable information, and that it does not sell or rent your contact information to third parties. That is the provider's own policy statement; we did not audit their servers or network. Separately, the pages carry Google AdSense advertising, which operates under Google's own data terms.

Access from India

In our test we opened the Mumbai page with prices in rupees and ran a Mumbai vs Delhi comparison, both of which worked. We did not test other Indian cities, and we did not test from an Indian internet connection, but we encountered no sign-up wall or gate during the session.

The verdict

Numbeo turns "I wonder what it costs to live there" into dated estimates in about ten seconds, across the 12,899 supported cities shown on the site today, Mumbai and Delhi among them. Free, instant and no account: numbeo.com
