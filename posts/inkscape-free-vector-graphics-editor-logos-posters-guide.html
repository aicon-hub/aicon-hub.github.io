Inkscape: free vector design tool for logos, posters and diagrams

By AiCoN | Published 16 October 2026 | Sources checked 16 October 2026

Inkscape describes itself as a free, open-source vector graphics editor for Windows, macOS and Linux, good for logos, posters and diagrams. Its privacy policy says the software collects no information.

What it is

Inkscape is a drawing program for vector graphics. Vector images are made from shapes and lines instead of pixels, so they stay sharp at any size. That makes it useful for logos, posters, icons, diagrams and clip art. Inkscape's About page calls it a "Free and open source vector graphics editor for GNU/Linux, Windows and macOS". It is not an AI tool. We included it as a free design tool. (We posted about Krita, a pixel-based painting program, on 15 October. Inkscape does a different job.)

Is it free?

Yes. Inkscape's home page calls it "a powerful, free design tool". The programme is released under the GNU General Public License, version 2, per its licensing page. Inkscape says it is a member of the Software Freedom Conservancy, a US non-profit, and that contributions are tax deductible in the United States. Its licence page also says files you save or export from Inkscape are owned by the creators of the work, that is you, and you choose the licence for your own work.

What you can do with it

  • Draw: the home page lists flexible drawing tools, a text tool and Bezier and spiro curves.
  • Make logos, posters and diagrams: the About page lists cartoons, clip art, logos, typography, diagramming and flowcharts.
  • Move files in and out: it says it can import and export SVG, AI, EPS, PDF, PS and PNG.
  • Add-ons: the About page says you can extend it with add-ons.
  • Where: GNU/Linux, Windows and macOS. The download page offers a Windows version for Arm chips.

What to watch out for

  • It is not a photo editor. For pixel-based photo work use a different program.
  • Version: the download page shows Inkscape 1.4.4, released 6 May 2026. A big 1.5 release is in the works, per the release notes, and the 1.4.4 release can convert the planned 1.5 multi-page file format back to the older one.
  • Known issues: the release notes list a macOS issue where extensions do not work if you start Inkscape from the command line, and a possible crash on some Wayland Linux systems when moving a docked tab.
  • The community provides help and learning material, and we did not check how good it is.

Privacy and data

  • Inkscape's privacy policy says "Inkscape, as a software, does not, in any way, collect or transmit any Personal Information (or any information at all)."
  • It adds a caveat: Inkscape can embed your computer username in file paths inside SVG files. If you share a file, it suggests saving as "Optimized SVG" to strip that out.
  • For its website, it says you can visit anonymously, and that it collects personal information such as a name and email address only if you submit it, for example when you register or fill in forms.

India

  • We saw no country limit on the download page. We did not test the download from India.

Frequently asked questions

Is Inkscape free?

Yes. It describes itself as a free design tool under the GPL.

Does it collect my data?

Its privacy policy says the software does not collect or transmit any information.

Who owns what I make?

Inkscape says the creators of the work own it.
