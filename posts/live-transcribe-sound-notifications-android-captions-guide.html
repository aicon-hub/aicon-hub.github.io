Live Transcribe: speech to text and sound alerts on Android

By AiCoN | 8 October 2026 | 5 min read

Live Transcribe turns speech and sound into text on your Android phone, and Sound Notifications alerts you to sounds like a smoke alarm. Google built it for people who are deaf or hard of hearing, and the app needs Android 12 or later.

What it is

Live Transcribe & Notifications is a free Google app for Android. Live Transcribe captures speech and sound and shows it as text on your screen in real time. Sound Notifications alerts you to sounds such as a smoke alarm or a crying baby. Google's Play listing says it was made with Gallaudet University for people who are deaf or hard of hearing. It also helps anyone who wants a live transcript of a conversation.

How to start

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Live Transcribe. On Pixel phones and some other phones it is already installed. Otherwise install it from Google Play.
  2. Accept the microphone permission.
  3. Hold the phone microphone near the person speaking.
  4. To reply, tap Type and write your answer on screen. You can also tap your typed message to enlarge it, or use Read aloud.

Useful features

LanguagesCurrent Play listing says over 120 languages and dialects. Check the language list on your phone; a published count is not a promise of every language on every device. You can set a primary and secondary language.
OfflineOn select Android devices and all Pixel phones on Android 12 or later, you can download several languages for offline use. On Android 8 to 11, English only. Without a download you need an internet connection.
Name alertYour phone can vibrate when someone says a name or word you choose. You can add custom words.
Sound NotificationsAlerts for sounds such as a smoke alarm or crying baby, with custom sounds for appliance beeps and a review of the past 12 hours.
Sign-to-textGoogle's help describes translating American Sign Language into English text using the front camera. It is in the help page, so check your phone for it. Google says to retype if a translation is wrong.

Privacy notes

Google Help says audio and transcriptions are encrypted and temporarily stored in the app, with history available for up to three days. It says turning history off deletes transcriptions after 24 hours. This differs from Play's default-not-saved wording; inspect the history setting rather than assuming immediate deletion.

  • Google's Play listing says transcriptions are not saved by default. You can choose to save them on your device for 3 days.
  • Google's Play listing says audio is not stored after it is processed.
  • Google's Sound Notifications help says audio stays on your device and nothing is sent to Google without your permission. Live Transcribe without an offline language download needs the internet, so check the app's privacy notice before using it for private talks.

Limits to know

Sound alerts can miss or misidentify events. Do not use them as a replacement for certified smoke alarms or other safety equipment.

  • The Play listing requires Android 12 or later.
  • It is not a certified medical or legal record. Check important words, names and numbers yourself.
  • Accuracy depends on noise, accents and microphone distance. We did not test accuracy.
  • It is free on Google Play as far as we can see. We did not check a regional price page, so we quote no INR price.

Frequently asked questions

Is it only for deaf or hard of hearing users?

Google made it for that community. Anyone can use it to read a conversation as text.

Does it work offline?

Only for languages you have downloaded, on supported devices.

Does it work on iPhone?

Google's pages describe Android only.

Does it support Hindi, Malayalam or Tamil?

We did not verify this. Check the language list in the app.
