Oracle Cloud Always Free Arm server: full step-by-step setup guide

By AiCoN | Published 18 October 2026 | Sources checked 18 October 2026

Oracle says its Always Free tier includes an Arm server with up to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB of memory. Here is the full setup, checked against Oracle docs. It needs a card.

What this is

Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) has an "Always Free" tier. Oracle's docs say Always Free resources are available for an unlimited time, in any account including trial accounts. One of them is an Arm server: the Ampere A1 shape, called VM.Standard.A1.Flex. This guide walks through setting one up, step by step, using only Oracle's own documentation. It is not an AI tool, but many people use a small always-on server for self-hosting, learning Linux or running bots.

Read this firstYou need a credit or debit card to sign up. Oracle says the card is used for identity verification and is not charged unless you upgrade. Always Free has no SLA, Oracle can reclaim idle instances, and capacity in your region can run out. We did not sign up or create a server. Everything below comes from Oracle's pages, read on 18 October 2026.

Corrections to what you may have read elsewhere

  • The limit is 2 OCPUs and 12 GB of memory in total, not 4 OCPUs and 24 GB. Oracle's Always Free page says Ampere A1 gets the first 1,500 OCPU hours and 9,000 GB hours per month, and that for Always Free tenancies this is equal to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB. You can use one instance with all of it, or up to two instances with 1 OCPU each, per the page.
  • Budgets have no "Tenancy" target. Oracle's Create Budget page lists Child Tenancy, Compartment and Tags as scopes. To cover everything, our reading is to choose the root compartment (the tenancy itself). That is our reading, not Oracle's wording.
  • The card hold: Oracle's billing page says the card is authorized for US$1 (or the local equivalent) at sign-up, and the authorization is reversed on Oracle's side. Your bank decides how long the reversal takes. Oracle's FAQ says holds are typically removed within 3 to 5 days. We found no India-specific statement.
  • "Paying gives you priority for capacity" is not stated by Oracle. What the docs say: if you hit an out-of-capacity error you can try another availability domain, wait and retry, or upgrade to Pay As You Go, which gives access to more compute resource types. Oracle's FAQ says Pay As You Go accounts are subject to different capacity limits than Always Free accounts.

Is it free?

  • Yes, within the limits. Oracle's FAQ says you are not charged for Always Free resources, and that after an upgrade to Pay As You Go you are only charged for usage above the Always Free limits.
  • The sign-up also gives a Free Trial with US$300 in credit (about 29,000 INR at 96.83 INR per US$) for up to 30 days, per Oracle. Always Free carries on after the trial ends.
  • If you go above 2 OCPUs and 12 GB of A1 while on the trial, Oracle says all your A1 instances are disabled and deleted 30 days after the trial ends unless you upgrade. Stay within the Always Free numbers to avoid that.
  • Always Free is not available in US Government regions. Accounts that only use Always Free are not eligible for Oracle Support, only the community forums, per Oracle.

What you get for the server (Oracle's numbers)

  • Ampere A1: 2 OCPUs and 12 GB in total, as above.
  • Storage: 200 GB in total of block volume, boot volumes included, plus five volume backups.
  • Boot volume size: Oracle's page is not consistent. One section gives a 47 GB minimum and another says 50 GB default and minimum. Plan for about 50 GB. A custom boot volume up to 200 GB uses all your storage.
  • Also free: up to two small AMD instances (VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro, 1/8 OCPU and 1 GB each), in one availability domain only.
  • Home region only: Always Free compute and block volumes must be created in your home region. A1 can be created in any availability domain in that region except South Korea North (Chuncheon), per Oracle.

Step 1. Sign up

  1. Have ready: an email address you can open, a mobile phone number, and a credit card or a debit card that works like one. Oracle's FAQ says it does not accept debit cards that need a PIN, or virtual, single-use or prepaid cards. Another Oracle page says a debit card with a major logo and no PIN can be used.
  2. Open Oracle's sign-up page from oracle.com/cloud/free. Choose your country, enter your name and email, and complete the CAPTCHA.
  3. Open the verification email and click the link. Oracle says it is valid for 30 minutes.
  4. Set a password. Oracle's rule: 8 to 40 characters with an upper-case letter, a lower-case letter, a number and a special character.
  5. Enter a Cloud Account Name. This becomes your tenancy name.
  6. Choose your Home Region carefully. Oracle says it cannot be changed after sign-up. Oracle's region list includes India West (Mumbai, ap-mumbai-1) and India South (Hyderabad, ap-hyderabad-1). Your Always Free server must live in this region, so pick the one you want to use. We could not verify which has more free A1 capacity.
  7. Accept the terms, then enter your address and phone number.
  8. Choose Add payment verification method, then Credit Card, and enter the card. Then choose Start my free trial.
  9. Oracle says one person or email can have one account. It prohibits multiple accounts. It also says accounts idle for 30 days or more may be treated as abandoned and suspended or ended.

Step 2. Set a budget alert (do this before anything else)

A budget is a soft limit. Oracle's docs say it sends alerts and does not stop spending. Oracle's Budgets page says alerts are evaluated every 24 hours, not immediately, so a budget is not a real-time charge guard. You need permission to manage budgets in the root compartment, which the first account user has as an administrator.

  1. Open the navigation menu, then Billing & Cost Management, then Budgets, then Create Budget.
  2. Give it a name and a monthly schedule. The minimum budget amount is 1 in the chosen currency. We did not verify which currency the console shows you.
  3. For the scope, choose Compartment and select the root compartment so it covers everything (our reading of Oracle's options).
  4. Create the budget, then add an alert rule. Choose the threshold metric Actual Spend (Forecast Spend needs 3 days of data), and a threshold type of Percentage or Absolute Amount. Oracle accepts a percentage above 0 up to 10,000.
  5. Add your email address as a recipient. Oracle says recipients are optional, so do not skip it.
  6. You cannot create a budget in a compartment that already has one.

Step 3. Create the network (VCN)

  1. Open the menu, then Networking, then Virtual cloud networks, then Start VCN Wizard.
  2. Choose Create VCN with Internet Connectivity. Name it and accept the defaults.
  3. This creates a public subnet and a Default Security List. You will edit that list in Step 6.

Step 4. Create the A1 instance

  1. Open the menu, then Compute, then Instances, then Create instance. Name it anything.
  2. Placement: the default availability domain (AD-1) is fine. Oracle's tutorial suggests the "Always Free Eligible" option for the availability domain. Keep on-demand capacity.
  3. Image: choose Edit, then Change image. Oracle lists Ubuntu 24.04 LTS and 22.04 LTS as images that support Arm-based shapes. For Arm, Oracle says to use the Ubuntu image, not Minimal Ubuntu. Oracle Linux is the other main choice. Look for the "Always Free Eligible" label.
  4. Shape: choose Change shape, then Virtual machine, then Ampere, then VM.Standard.A1.Flex. Set OCPUs and memory. Up to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB keeps you inside the free limit. One instance with 2 and 12, or two instances with 1 OCPU each.
  5. Networking: pick the VCN and the public subnet from Step 3, and set Assign a public IPv4 address to Yes.
  6. SSH keys: choose Generate a key pair for me, and download both the private and public key. Oracle says you cannot get them again later. Keep the private key safe.
  7. Boot volume: leave the default size, or set a custom size up to 200 GB if you want all your free storage on this server. Leave in-transit encryption on, as the tutorial does.
  8. Choose Create. Provisioning takes several minutes. Oracle says to wait a few more minutes after it shows Running before you connect.
  9. Copy the instance's public IP address from the details page.

Step 5. Connect with SSH

The user name is ubuntu for Ubuntu images and opc for Oracle Linux, per Oracle.

Mac or Linux:

chmod 400 ~/Downloads/ssh-key.key
ssh -i ~/Downloads/ssh-key.key ubuntu@YOUR_PUBLIC_IP

Windows: Oracle says you can use the OpenSSH client in PowerShell, with file permissions on the private key set so only you can read it. The command is the same: ssh -i path\to\ssh-key.key ubuntu@YOUR_PUBLIC_IP. Type yes when asked to accept the fingerprint.

Once in, update packages. Oracle's docs give these for Ubuntu images:

sudo apt-get update
sudo apt-get upgrade

Step 6. Open web ports (two firewalls)

Traffic must pass the cloud network rule and the firewall inside the server. Opening only one will not work.

6a. Cloud rule (security list). Go to Networking, Virtual cloud networks, your VCN, your public subnet, then Default Security List, then Add Ingress Rules.

  • Source CIDR: 0.0.0.0/0 (open to the whole internet)
  • IP protocol: TCP
  • Destination port: 80. Repeat for 443 if you will serve HTTPS.

Official Oracle tutorial screenshot hosted by Oracle. Oracle tutorial: Create a virtual cloud network. Independent guide, not affiliated or sponsored. Screenshot and trademarks belong to their owners.

Oracle's Apache tutorial also ticks "Stateless" on its rule. Oracle's page for security lists documents the rule fields. We would leave Stateless off unless you know why you need it, but this is our view, not Oracle's.

6b. Server firewall (iptables). Oracle's Ubuntu tutorial says the Ubuntu firewall is disabled by default but iptables still has to be updated. Its commands for port 80:

sudo iptables -I INPUT 6 -m state --state NEW -p tcp --dport 80 -j ACCEPT
sudo netfilter-persistent save

For HTTPS, the same pattern with --dport 443 follows from this, but Oracle's tutorial only shows port 80. The package name iptables-persistent and the netfilter-persistent reload command are common advice we did not find on an Oracle page, so treat them as unverified. Also, Oracle's image documentation says not to use UFW to edit rules on Ubuntu images, because it can stop the instance from booting. Use iptables as above.

Do not remove the default rules for the boot and block volume network (169.254.x.x port 3260). Oracle recommends keeping them.

Step 7. If you get "Out of capacity"

This is common for free Arm servers. Oracle's docs describe it as a temporary lack of Always Free shapes in your home region that can take several days to clear. What Oracle suggests:

  1. Try a different availability domain in your home region, if your region has more than one.
  2. Wait and try again later.
  3. Upgrade to Pay As You Go. Oracle says it gives access to more compute resource types and that you are not charged for Always Free resources, only for use above the limits. Remember this needs the card, and the upgrade authorizes US$100 (or local equivalent) on the card, reversed on Oracle's side per its billing page. Oracle says the upgrade can take a day or two.

Another option is the free AMD micro instance, which is easier to get but is small (1 GB).

Step 8. Keep the server from being reclaimed

Oracle says idle Always Free compute instances may be reclaimed. An instance counts as idle if, over a 7-day period, all of these are true: CPU use at the 95th percentile is under 20%, network use is under 20%, and (for A1 only) memory use is under 20%. So do not leave a completely unused server. Back up anything important, because Always Free has no SLA.

Step 9. Optional: add a block volume

Oracle's tutorial shows how to create a block volume, attach it as paravirtualized, then partition and mount it with fdisk. It counts against the same 200 GB total.

Step 10. If you stop using it

Terminate the instance from its details page. Check the options on that screen about the boot volume, because storage left behind counts toward your 200 GB. We did not verify the exact wording of that screen. Keep the budget alert from Step 2 switched on.

Privacy and data

  • Oracle collects your name, email, phone, address and card details at sign-up. We did not read Oracle's full privacy policy for this guide.
  • Your server is yours to secure. Oracle's docs say you are responsible for OS security updates on Ubuntu images.

India

  • Oracle lists Mumbai and Hyderabad as regions. We did not verify that sign-up works with every Indian card, or the card-hold amount in India.
  • No INR price applies for Always Free, which costs nothing. We did not quote any Pay As You Go prices.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Oracle Always Free Arm server really free?

Yes, within 2 OCPUs, 12 GB of memory and 200 GB of storage, per Oracle. You need a card to sign up.

Can I get 4 OCPUs and 24 GB?

Oracle's Always Free page says 2 OCPUs and 12 GB for Always Free tenancies.

Can I change my home region later?

No. Oracle says it cannot be changed after sign-up.

Will I be charged?

Oracle says your card is not charged unless you upgrade, and then only for usage above the free limits.
