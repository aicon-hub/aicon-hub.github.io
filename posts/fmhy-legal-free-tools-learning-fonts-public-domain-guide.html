FMHY: the legal side of the internet's biggest free-stuff directory

By Muhammed Salman | Published 10 November 2026 | Sources checked 10 November 2026

FMHY (freemediaheckyeah) is a volunteer-run wiki that calls itself the largest collection of free stuff on the internet. Part of it is genuinely useful and fully legal: lists of open-source tools, free courses, fonts, stock media and public-domain books. Another part lists pirated content. AiCoN does not support piracy and does not link to those pages. This guide covers only the legal side, with every named pick checked on its own official site.

Read this first

FMHY has sections for streaming, torrents, cracked software and paid books offered for free. That is piracy: it is illegal in India and it harms the people who make those things. We do not support it, and this guide does not link to any of those pages. The only FMHY link in this guide is its homepage, fmhy.net, so you can open the legal sections yourself. If you use pirated services, you also carry the malware and data risk that comes with them.

Quick facts

  • What it is: a community-edited directory of free things on the internet, at fmhy.net. Volunteers edit it through GitHub.
  • The honest catch: the same wiki indexes pirated streaming, torrent and cracked-software pages. They exist, and we do not link or recommend them.
  • What is legal and useful: its lists of open-source software, free courses, fonts, stock images and audio, and public-domain reading point at genuinely free, legal projects.
  • Entries are community-submitted. A star next to an entry means the FMHY community vets it, not that AiCoN does. Prefer a tool's official site before you download anything.
  • Checked by AiCoN: we read fmhy.net and checked every tool named below on its own official site on 10 November 2026. We did not open the piracy sections.

Five legal picks, each verified on its official site

  1. The Odin Project (theodinproject.com): a free full-stack web development curriculum. The official site says it is free and supported by a passionate open source community.
  2. roadmap.sh (roadmap.sh): community-curated developer roadmaps, role-based and skill-based. Its about page says the project is open source and among the most-starred projects on GitHub.
  3. Fontshare (fontshare.com): professional fonts that are 100% free for personal and commercial use. It is run by the Indian Type Foundry in Ahmedabad, the team behind Poppins, one of the most used fonts on the web.
  4. Standard Ebooks (standardebooks.org): public-domain classics with professional typography and proper covers, free of cost, made by volunteers.
  5. Openverse (openverse.org): a search engine for more than 800 million Creative Commons images and audio files you can legally reuse, with the licence shown per item.

Many other tools FMHY lists already have their own AiCoN guides, including GIMP, freeCodeCamp, Google Fonts, OBS Studio, LibreOffice, Photopea, Krita, Blender, VLC, MIT OpenCourseWare, Project Gutenberg and LibriVox.

How to browse it safely

  • Open only the legal sections: Educational, and the software, fonts, stock-media and public-domain reading lists.
  • Download software from the official site of the tool, not from a mirror or aggregator the wiki lists.
  • Never install cracked or patched apps. Beyond being illegal, they are a common way to get malware.
  • The FMHY volunteers also maintain a browser extension called FMHY SafeGuard that marks links the community found unsafe. It is optional, and it is their judgement, not ours.

Limits and things to know

  • AiCoN did not browse or verify the piracy sections, and we will not answer questions about them.
  • Community wikis change daily. A link that was safe last month can rot. If a page looks wrong, leave it.
  • The "largest collection" line is FMHY's own claim, not something we measured.
