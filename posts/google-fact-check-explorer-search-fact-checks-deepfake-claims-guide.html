Google Fact Check Explorer: check a viral claim against fact-checkers

By AiCoN | Published 27 October 2026 | Sources checked and tested 27 October 2026

Fake videos and doctored clips now spread faster than most of us can check them. Google Fact Check Explorer, part of Google's Fact Check Tools, lets you search a claim or topic and see which fact-checking publishers have reviewed it and what rating each gave. It is a search over published fact-check articles, not an AI chatbot, so it points you to the publisher's own write-up. We tested it on 27 October 2026 with two topic searches and a publisher filter. We found no payment page, and no payment was requested in our test.

Quick facts

  • Google's FAQ says you can search by keywords, from specific topics to full quotes, and see matching claims with their fact checks. Results can be limited by language and by publisher.
  • Per the FAQ, a fact check appears only if the publisher marked it up in the ClaimReview format and it meets Google's guidelines for Search or YouTube. A claim with no result may simply not be covered; it does not mean the claim is true.
  • Google says it does not endorse or create the fact checks. Disagreements go to the website that published the check.
  • The FAQ says you can also search by image, using an image URL or an upload, while a separate image context tab is currently limited to beta testers who apply. We did not test image search.
  • The FAQ names a Google account only for the Markup Tool (for publishers) and the image beta. It does not say whether text search needs one. Our test browser was already signed in to a Google account, so we did not verify signed-out use.
  • We found no payment page. For data handling, Google points to its general privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy; we did not review it for this post.

What makes it useful

Explorer lists the reviews that publishers have filed, each with the publisher's name, a rating, a date and a link to the article, so you can read the evidence yourself and see who made the call. That is its value. It checks only claims that fact-checkers have already reviewed.

Our test

On 27 October 2026 we opened toolbox.google.com/factcheck/explorer in a desktop browser.

  • Topic search: we searched "deepfake video india". The first results were claims reviewed by BOOM Fact Check and Thai PBS, rated "False" and "Disinformation", plus an AFP Fact Check item and a Full Fact item, each with a link to the original article and a date.
  • Second topic search: we searched "moon landing". The top results were claims rated "False" by Snopes and AFP Fact Check and a Full Fact write-up with its explanation, each dated and linked to the publisher's article. The screenshot below shows this search.
  • Publisher filter: we searched "site:boomlive.in deepfake", the format the FAQ describes. The first four results we read all came from BOOM Fact Check. The newest was a claim about an Indian Army officer, marked "False" and dated "23 hours ago" at the time.
  • Not tested: image search by URL or upload, the language filter beyond English, the Markup Tool, the API, signed-out use, mobile use, or how complete the coverage is. Three searches show the tool working; they do not show it finds a review for every claim.

How to try it

  1. Open toolbox.google.com/factcheck/explorer.
  2. Type a claim, a name or a topic into the search box. Use the quoted claim if you have one.
  3. Read the rating and the date on each result, then open the link to read the publisher's reasoning.
  4. To see one publisher only, add site: and its domain, for example site:boomlive.in. Use the language filter if you want other languages.
  5. No result means no matching published review was found. Try different words, or check the claim with other sources.

Availability: Google's FAQ gives no country restriction and we found no India-specific statement. We did not check this from India, and our test does not show how it behaves from your location.
