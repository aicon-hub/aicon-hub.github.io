Musicca: learn music theory free, in your browser

By AiCoN | Published 21 October 2026 | Sources checked 21 October 2026

Musicca teaches music theory for free in your browser: note-reading exercises, a virtual piano, a metronome, a tuner and more, with no account needed. We ran one of its exercises ourselves. Here is what its pages say and what we found.

Quick facts

  • What it does: free music theory exercises (notes, rhythms, intervals, chords, scales, key signatures), virtual instruments (piano, guitar, drums and more), and tools like a metronome, tuner and chord finder.
  • Price: free. Its mission page says "For free, forever." An optional free account saves progress and joins the leaderboard. Ads support the site, per its privacy policy.
  • Platforms: any modern web browser, in about 20 languages including Hindi.
  • Best for: beginners learning to read music, students, and anyone who wants a quick metronome or tuner without installing an app.

What it is

Musicca is a music learning website from a small company in Copenhagen, Denmark. Its mission statement reads: "Our mission is to give people around the world the opportunity to learn music theory. For free, forever." The same page says millions of people from more than 150 countries use it every month.

We tried it

This was a functional workflow test with scripted clicks: it checks that the exercise works start to finish, not how it feels to learn with it.

  • Opened the Notes exercise (Piano, Naturals 1) on musicca.com on 21 October 2026, without an account.
  • Pressed Start and answered six questions. Each question marked a key on an on-screen piano and offered seven answer buttons (C through B); every answer registered and the progress bar advanced.
  • No login, payment or download wall appeared at any point.

Is it free?

Yes. The site says "For free, forever" on its mission page, and we hit no paywall in our test. The site shows advertisements, and its privacy policy names BuySellAds as its advertising provider. The optional account is free.

Privacy and data

  • Visiting the site shares anonymized statistics (country, browser, pages visited), per the privacy policy updated 8 April 2026.
  • An account collects your name, email, age, country and password; a minor's account also collects a parent's email. Exercise progress is stored when logged in.
  • Cookies are used to show relevant ads; the policy says personally identifiable information is not used for advertising.

Our advice

  • Start with Notes then Rhythms under Exercises; ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week. This is our advice, not the site's.
  • The metronome and tuner under Tools are handy even if you never take a lesson.

India

  • It is a website, so it works anywhere, and Hindi is one of its listed languages. Our test connection was not an Indian one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Musicca really free?

Yes. Its own mission page says free forever, it is supported by ads, and the optional account is free. We found no paywall.

Do I need an account?

No. Exercises, instruments and tools work without one. An account only saves progress and enables the leaderboard.

Is it good for children?

Its privacy policy has a children's privacy section and collects a parent's email for a minor's account. The content itself is music education.
