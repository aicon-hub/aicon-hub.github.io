Ezgif: free online GIF maker and editor, no sign-up

By AiCoN | Published 21 October 2026 | Sources checked 21 October 2026

Ezgif.com is a free, no-signup online toolset for making and editing animated GIFs and converting between image and video formats. Here is what its own pages say about limits, file retention and ads, and what happened when we uploaded a test GIF and ran its optimizer.

Quick facts

  • What it does: makes, edits, converts and optimizes animated GIFs in the browser, plus WebP, APNG, MNG, JXL and AVIF animations, basic video editing and many file converters. Its most popular tools are the GIF maker, the Video to GIF converter and the animated image resizer, per its about page.
  • Price: free. The about page calls it "a free, simple to use toolset" and we found no paid plan or sign-in system on the pages we checked. It is ad-supported.
  • Account: none. There is no registration or sign-in on the site.
  • Limits: uploads are capped at 200MB per file on the tools we saw, and the site's policy says uploaded files are kept for up to 1 hour after last use, then deleted. Some tools offer a "local processing" checkbox that keeps the file in your browser with no size limit.
  • Made by: the about page presents Ezgif as a one-developer project with a single contact email. The site runs on open-source components including ImageMagick, gifsicle, FFmpeg and gifski.
  • Best for: quick GIF jobs without installing anything: shrink a GIF for WhatsApp, turn a short clip into a GIF, crop, reverse, add text or split frames.

What it is

Ezgif.com describes itself as a simple, free online GIF maker and toolset for basic animated image editing, covering GIF, APNG, WebP, MNG, JXL and AVIF. Beyond animation tools it hosts dozens of converters (video to GIF, GIF to MP4, image format converters, PDF tools and more) and basic video editing. The site is actively maintained: its news log shows updates on 11, 14, 16 and 20 October 2026, including a new "local processing" mode that runs some tools entirely in your browser and a multi-track video timeline editor.

What we tested

On 21 October 2026 we ran a scripted functional test through the site's own upload form, with no account. We generated a small 4-frame animated GIF locally (320x240, 8.02KiB), uploaded it to the GIF optimizer, and the site correctly reported its size, dimensions, frame count and length. We then submitted "Optimize GIF!" with the default settings (lossy 15, fuzz 3). The tool processed the file and returned an "Optimized image" result of 8.07KiB, marked +0.61% - slightly larger, which is a fair outcome on a tiny already-simple test file, and the tool honestly reports it instead of hiding it. A Save link and further edit options (crop, resize, effects, convert) were offered on the result page. This was a scripted test of one workflow (upload + optimize), not a full review of every tool; we did not test video tools, local processing mode or large files.

Is it free?

Yes, per its own pages. The about page calls ezgif "a free, simple to use toolset", the homepage titles it "free online animated GIF editor", and no page we checked shows a paid plan, subscription or credit system. The site pays for itself with advertisements: the privacy policy says cookies are used to serve ads, alongside anonymized, aggregated traffic analytics and remembering preferences.

Privacy and data

  • The privacy policy says the site does not knowingly collect or store personal information on its servers.
  • Uploaded files are stored for up to 1 hour after they are last used, then automatically deleted; filenames are not listed publicly.
  • Cookies are used for anonymized, aggregated analytics, remembering preferences and serving advertisements.
  • On tools that offer it, a "local processing" checkbox processes the file in your browser only, so it is never uploaded. The October 2026 news entries say local mode also removes file size limits.
  • The policy invites removal requests if anything hosted contains your personal data or content.

India

  • No region restriction appears on the pages we read, and the tool worked normally in our test. We did not test from India itself.
  • Everything is free, so there is no pricing to convert.

Good to know

  • Uploads are limited to 200MB per file (shown on the upload form) and the site says they are deleted up to 1 hour after last use - download your result right away.
  • Optimization is not magic: on our small test GIF the optimizer returned a file 0.61% larger and reported it plainly. Simple or already-optimized files may not shrink.
  • The site is run as a one-developer project with an email contact and a Reddit community, so support is informal.
  • An FAQ entry asks "Does ezgif have an API?" - we did not read the answer page, so treat automation as unsupported by us.

Frequently asked questions

Is ezgif free?

Yes. Its about page calls it a free toolset, we found no paid plan or sign-in on the pages checked, and the site is ad-supported.

Do I need an account?

No. The site has no registration or sign-in system at all.

What happens to the files I upload?

Per the privacy policy and the upload form, files are stored on its servers for up to 1 hour after last use and then automatically deleted; filenames are not listed publicly. On tools with a "local processing" checkbox, the file stays in your browser and is never uploaded.

What can it handle besides GIF?

Animated WebP, APNG, AVIF, MNG and JXL, basic video editing, and many one-off converters such as Video to GIF, GIF to MP4, WebP/JPG/PNG conversions and PDF tools, per its tool lists.
