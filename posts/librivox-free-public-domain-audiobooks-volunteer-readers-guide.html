LibriVox: free public-domain audiobooks read by volunteers

By AiCoN | Published 16 October 2026 | Sources checked 16 October 2026

LibriVox says its audiobooks of public-domain books are free, read by volunteers, and the project is ad-free. Third-party apps that use them are not run by LibriVox.

What it is

LibriVox is a library of free audiobooks. Its About page says its objective is "to make all books in the public domain available, narrated by real people and distributed for free, in audio format on the internet". Volunteers record the chapters. It is not an AI tool. We included it as a free listening resource.

Is it free?

Yes. LibriVox says its audiobooks are free, and calls itself "a non-commercial, non-profit and ad-free project". The recordings are in the public domain, so LibriVox says you may use them "for whatever purpose you wish". The page carries an asterisk after "free" and we did not find the footnote, so check the site if you plan to reuse recordings.

What you can do with it

  • Browse the catalog and download books from the website. LibriVox says the original catalog recordings are "always free and never include ads".
  • Subscribe to its podcast: LibriVox says it podcasts one book at a time, with three installments a week.
  • Read along with the text: LibriVox says most of its texts come from Project Gutenberg.
  • Volunteer to read: LibriVox says no experience is needed and it accepts volunteers in all languages.

What to watch out for

  • Old books. LibriVox says many of the books are very old and may contain language or expressions we would not use today.
  • Volunteer voices. The readers are volunteers, so the sound quality and style vary.
  • Third-party apps are not LibriVox. LibriVox says all apps for its recordings are made by third parties, and some sell the recordings or add ads. Its 2016 blog post says the originals on its catalog are always free and ad-free. We did not test any app.
  • The catalog is mostly older titles. It holds public-domain books only, so do not expect recent bestsellers.

Privacy and data

  • We did not find a privacy page for LibriVox on the pages we read, so we cannot tell you what data the site or its forum collects. Downloading does not appear to need an account, but we did not confirm that.
  • LibriVox says it is ad-free. Third-party apps have their own privacy practices.

India

  • LibriVox says it welcomes volunteers "from across the globe, in all languages". We did not find a page saying anything about India. It is a website, so try it from your browser. No price is involved. LibriVox says its files are public domain in the USA, but not necessarily everywhere in the world. Check local copyright rules before downloading or reusing a recording in India.

Frequently asked questions

Is LibriVox free?

Yes, per LibriVox. It says the recordings are free, in the public domain, and the project is ad-free.

Does it have its own app?

We did not find an official LibriVox app. LibriVox says apps are made by third parties.

Can I use the recordings in my own project?

LibriVox says they are public domain and may be used for any purpose. Its listening page clarifies that the files are public domain in the USA, but not necessarily everywhere in the world. Check the copyright rules where you live before reusing them.
