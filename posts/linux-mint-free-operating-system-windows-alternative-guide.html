Linux Mint: a free operating system for your PC

By AiCoN | Published 17 October 2026 | Sources checked 17 October 2026

Linux Mint is a free operating system for desktops and laptops. Its home page says completely free of cost and no data collection. Back up before you install.

What it is

Linux Mint is a free operating system for desktop and laptop computers, an alternative to Windows. Its home page says it is "designed to work 'out of the box' and comes fully equipped with the apps most people need", and that most of its users come from Windows. The page lists LibreOffice, Firefox, GIMP, Blender and Inkscape among its included tools. It is not an AI tool. We included it as a free way to put a new life into a computer.

Is it free?

Yes, per the home page: "It is completely free of cost and almost all of its components are Open Source." It is based on Debian and Ubuntu. The site has a donors page, so it accepts support, but we saw no price.

What you can do with it

  • Everyday work. The home page says it includes the LibreOffice office suite and Firefox for browsing, and can import from or export to PDF or Microsoft Office documents.
  • Media and games. The home page mentions music, TV, movies, podcasts, and Steam with more than 7,800 games.
  • Low maintenance. The home page says it has centralized software updates and system snapshots.
  • Try before installing. The installation guide says you can try to boot Linux Mint on your computer, and if it is not compatible, "nothing bad will happen" and you get an error message.

What to watch out for

  • Installing can wipe your disk. The installation guide describes an "Erase disk and install Linux Mint" option for when "all data can be lost on the hard drive". Back up your files first and read the guide before you install.
  • You make an installer first. The guide says Linux Mint comes as an ISO file that you use to make a bootable DVD or USB stick.
  • Windows-only programs. We did not check which Windows programs run on it. Check that the apps you rely on have a Linux version.
  • Not tested. We did not download or install it.

Privacy and data

  • The home page says "We don't collect data, we don't work against you."
  • The privacy page says the team is committed to gathering as little personal data as possible, ideally none, and stores personal data only when needed for its services or the law.
  • The website and its services have their own cookies and policy, described on that page.

India

  • It is free, so no rupee price is involved. The site mentions no country limits. We did not check download speed or mirrors from India.

Frequently asked questions

Is Linux Mint free?

Yes, per its home page: completely free of cost.

Will it erase my files?

It can, if you choose the erase disk option. Back up first.

Does it collect my data?

The home page says it does not collect data.
