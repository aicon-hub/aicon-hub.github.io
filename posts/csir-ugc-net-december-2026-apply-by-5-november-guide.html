CSIR NET December 2026: apply by 5 November, negative marking applies

By AiCoN | Published 24 October 2026 | Sources checked 24 October 2026

Online applications for the Joint CSIR-UGC NET December 2026 close on Thursday, 5 November 2026, at 11:50 PM on csirnet.nta.nic.in. This is the science-stream counterpart of UGC NET: it decides Junior Research Fellowship (JRF), Assistant Professor eligibility and PhD admission across five science subjects. The computer-based exam runs on Saturday, 19 December, Tuesday, 22 December and Sunday, 27 December 2026, in two shifts. One important difference from UGC NET: this exam has negative marking, so blind guessing costs marks.

Key dates from the official notice

  • Application window: 7 October 2026 to Thursday, 5 November 2026, up to 11:50 PM.
  • Last date to pay the fee: 5 November 2026, 11:50 PM.
  • Late submission with Rs 500 late fee: 6 November to Monday, 9 November 2026, 11:50 PM.
  • Correction window: 10-12 November 2026, on the website only.
  • Exam dates: 19, 22 and 27 December 2026. Two shifts: 9 AM to 12 noon and 3 PM to 6 PM.
  • Exam city intimation and admit card dates: to be announced on the website.

These dates are from the NTA public notice dated 7 October 2026, also hosted by CSIR's Human Resource Development Group, and the December 2026 Information Bulletin on csirnet.nta.nic.in.

Subjects covered

Five subjects: Chemical Sciences; Earth, Atmospheric, Ocean and Planetary Sciences; Life Sciences and Bio-Technology; Mathematical Sciences; and Physical Sciences. If your post-graduation is outside these, the main UGC NET (87 subjects, applications closing 28 October) is the parallel exam to check.

Who can apply

  • Master's degree or equivalent with at least 55% marks (without rounding off) for General and General-EWS candidates.
  • At least 50% marks (without rounding off) for OBC-NCL, SC, ST, PwD/PwBD and Third Gender candidates.
  • Final-year Master's students and those awaiting results can apply provisionally.
  • Four-year (8-semester) bachelor's degree holders with at least 75% aggregate (5% relaxation for reserved categories) can apply for JRF and PhD admission only - this route does not make you eligible for Assistant Professor.

Age limits

For JRF, the limit is 30 years as on 1 December 2026, with up to five years' relaxation for OBC-NCL, SC, ST, PwD/PwBD and Third Gender candidates and for women, plus limited relaxation for research experience. There is no upper age limit for Assistant Professor or for PhD admission only.

Exam pattern - and the negative marking

The test is a single three-hour paper of 200 marks in three parts (A, B and C), all multiple choice, in English and Hindi. Each subject has its own question split - for example, Chemical Sciences sets 120 questions and lets you attempt up to 75. Wrong answers lose marks, and the penalty depends on your subject: Chemical Sciences, Life Sciences and Physical Sciences deduct 25% in all three parts; Earth Sciences deducts 25% in Parts A and B and 33% in Part C; Mathematical Sciences deducts 25% in Parts A and B but has no negative marking in Part C.

Application fee

Rs 1,150 for General and unreserved candidates; Rs 600 for General-EWS and OBC-NCL; Rs 325 for SC, ST, PwD/PwBD and Third Gender candidates. Payment is online only - credit card, debit card, net banking or UPI - and bank or gateway service charges may apply. The late window adds Rs 500.

How to apply

  1. Open csirnet.nta.nic.in and use the December 2026 application link under Candidate Activity.
  2. Register and fill the form, choosing your subject and exam cities carefully.
  3. Upload your photo and signature as the bulletin specifies.
  4. Pay the fee online before 11:50 PM on 5 November 2026.
  5. Download the confirmation page after payment succeeds. NTA says candidates need not send any certificates or printouts to it.
  6. Use the 10-12 November correction window only for genuine mistakes.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not confuse this with the main UGC NET - they are different portals and different subject lists, and the UGC NET deadline (28 October) is earlier. Budget for the negative marking in your preparation: unlike UGC NET, unanswered questions are safer than guessed ones here. And finish the payment step; the form counts only after a successful transaction by the deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Is there negative marking in CSIR NET?

Yes, in most parts. Chemical, Life Sciences and Physical Sciences deduct 25% in all parts; Earth Sciences deducts 25% in Parts A and B and 33% in Part C; Mathematical Sciences has no negative marking in Part C.

Is there an age limit for Assistant Professor eligibility?

No. The 30-year limit applies only to JRF.

Can I apply for both UGC NET and CSIR NET?

They are separate exams on separate portals with separate fees. Science postgraduates typically check which subject list their degree fits; some candidates appear in both cycles.
