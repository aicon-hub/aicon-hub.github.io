OpenBoard: a free interactive whiteboard for teachers

By AiCoN | Published 9 November 2026 | Sources checked 9 November 2026

OpenBoard is a free whiteboard program for teaching. A teacher can write, draw and annotate on screen during a lesson, with an interactive board, a pen tablet or just a mouse. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux.

Quick facts

  • What it is: open source interactive whiteboard software for schools and universities.
  • Price and sign-up: the official site says it is free and open source under the GPLv3 licence, and that no registration is required.
  • Platforms: Windows, macOS and Linux. There is no phone or tablet app listed on the site.
  • Who maintains it: the Education Department (DIP) of the canton of Geneva in Switzerland, with community work on GitHub, according to the OpenBoard site.
  • Origin: it was forked from Open-Sankore 2.0, which was based on Uniboard.
  • Checked by AiCoN: we read the OpenBoard home page on 9 November 2026. We did not install or run it.

How can a teacher use it?

  • Write and draw on the screen while teaching, instead of using a chalkboard.
  • Use a pen tablet, an interactive whiteboard, or a mouse. The site says handwriting is a focus for the project.
  • Use a dual screen set-up: the pen tablet or laptop for you, the projector for the class.
  • The site describes it for use from school level to university.

How to start

  1. Open openboard.ch and use the download link for your system.
  2. Install it and open a new document.
  3. Try the pen tool on a blank page before class. Test your projector or board first.

Limits and things to know

  • AiCoN did not install OpenBoard. We cannot say how well it works with your pen tablet or classroom board.
  • It is a desktop program, so it needs a computer in the class.
  • It is community software. Support comes from the community and the project pages, not from a company.
  • Check that your school computers allow you to install software before you plan a lesson around it.

Sources: OpenBoard official site. Independent guide by AiCoN - not affiliated with OpenBoard. Checked 9 November 2026. Details can change; check the official source before you act.
