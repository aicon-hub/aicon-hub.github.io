Hack for Social Cause 2027: students 18-29, cash prizes up to ₹75,000

By AiCoN | Published 13 October 2026 | Sources checked 13 October 2026

Hack for Social Cause is a national technology challenge for students aged 18 to 29, run by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports under the Viksit Bharat Young Leaders Dialogue (VBYLD) 2027, with IIT Bombay as knowledge partner. The top 5 teams at the national showcase in New Delhi get cash prizes, from ₹75,000 for first place. Entry is free.

Deadline rechecked 24 October 2026: the public challenge page displays Registration Open. Its current timeline and the VBYLD tracks table both list 27 October 2026 for Stage-I registration and submission. However, the HSC FAQ still says 15 October. The timeline has been updated but the FAQ conflict has not been fully removed. Confirm with MY Bharat before relying on entry acceptance; we did not log in or test a submission. Dedicated page · VBYLD tracks.

Cash prizes (top 5 teams at the national showcase)

  • First: ₹75,000
  • Second: ₹50,000
  • Third: ₹25,000
  • Fourth: ₹15,000
  • Fifth: ₹15,000

National finalists also get certificates of excellence, and the page lists links to Startup India and incubation programmes. The 36 national finalist teams get online mentoring from IIT Bombay. The FAQ also lists a ₹6,000 prototype development grant for each national finalist team and travel support under applicable MoYAS norms. Cash prizes are subject to tax deductions and may be paid to the Team Lead's registered bank account. These are listed benefits, not an AiCoN guarantee of payment.

Who can enter

  • Indian citizens aged 18 to 29 as of 17 August 2026
  • Currently enrolled in a college, university or polytechnic. The page says AICTE-registered in one place and AISHE-registered in another, so check that your institution qualifies
  • Teams of up to 3 students, or enter alone. Each student can be in only one team
  • Every member registers individually on MY Bharat, then the Team Lead registers the team under the Hack for Social Cause track
  • HSC 2026 national finalists and winners cannot enter the 2027 national round

What to submit

  • A 6 to 7 slide deck on the IIT Bombay template (PPT or PDF, up to 10 MB), covering the problem, solution, impact and implementation, plus a disclosure of how much AI tools were used
  • A public GitHub or GitLab repository with complete open-source code, a README with setup steps, architecture notes, sample data or tests, and licence information (FAQ Q21)
  • A 3 to 5 minute demo video (preferred, not mandatory): MP4/MOV, at least 720p, up to 80 MB
  • Team details, Annexure 1 signed self-certification and a self-attested Student ID Card for each member. The FAQ also asks for government photo ID details at submission

Themes

There are 11 indicative themes, including rural development, education and skills, healthcare, governance and civic tech, environment, digital inclusion, digital safety and cyber fraud awareness, elderly care and assistive technology. You can adapt them to a local problem.

What happens next

  • The current HSC timeline and VBYLD tracks agree on state/regional rounds: 7 November to 5 December 2026. The HSC FAQ still says 26 October to 30 November. Confirm the actual schedule with the organiser.
  • The updated HSC timeline lists screening for national finalists on 5 to 16 December 2026; its FAQ still has older dates. Three teams per State/UT advance to screening, with one selected for each State/UT.
  • Mentoring remains inconsistent: the HSC timeline now lists 17 December 2026 to 7 January 2027, while the VBYLD tracks table lists 10 December 2026 to 7 January 2027. The HSC FAQ has still older wording.
  • National showcase and awards in New Delhi: 10 to 12 January 2027, as currently listed.

Rules worth knowing

  • Participation is free at every stage
  • The VBYLD tracks page says participants may enter only one Innovate for Bharat challenge. Check this before also entering Design for Bharat or Youth Entrepreneurs Challenge
  • You keep the intellectual property in your entry. Organisers get a non-exclusive licence to display and promote it with credit, and the FAQ requires a fully open-source public code repository
  • Team names must be respectful and not based on a person, place or community

How to apply: step by step

Rechecked on 24 October 2026: the public page displays Registration Open, and both current timeline tables list 27 October 2026 for Stage-I. The HSC FAQ still gives 15 October. These are published instructions, not confirmation that your logged-in submission will be accepted.

  1. Open the official page: mybharat.gov.in/pages/hack-for-social-cause. Entry is free. Confirm your team is eligible: Indian citizens aged 18-29 as of 17 August 2026, currently enrolled in a qualifying institution. The eligibility panel says AICTE-registered; the FAQ says AISHE-registered. Confirm your institution qualifies before preparing an entry.
  2. Every team member (up to 3, same or different institutions) registers individually on the MY Bharat portal first.
  3. Choose one Team Lead, then tap NEW REGISTRATION on the page and register the team in the Hack for Social Cause track.
  4. Prepare the submission: a state-specific problem statement, a 6-7 slide presentation deck (use the IIT Bombay template from the submission page), a public code repository link, an optional 3-5 minute demo video (preferred, not mandatory per FAQ), and the signed team self-declaration (Annexure 1 with IDs and the AI-use disclosure).
  5. The current timeline asks the Team Lead to submit all documents and the solution by 27 October 2026. Confirm the deadline and portal acceptance because the FAQ still says 15 October; no cutoff time was established.
  6. State and regional competitions run 7 November to 5 December 2026; top teams reach the national showcase at VBYLD 2027 in New Delhi (10-12 January 2027).

Frequently asked questions

Is entry free?

The HSC FAQ says participation is free at every stage. Any travel support is subject to MoYAS norms.

Which deadline should I follow?

The updated HSC timeline and VBYLD tracks table now both list 27 October 2026, and the public page displays Registration Open. The HSC FAQ still says 15 October. Confirm with MY Bharat; the public label is not a tested submission or eligibility guarantee.

Can I enter alone?

Yes. The FAQ allows an individual entry or a team of up to three students.
