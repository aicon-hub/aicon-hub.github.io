Instructables: 135,000+ free step-by-step DIY project guides

By AiCoN | Published 22 October 2026 | Sources checked 22 October 2026

From cardboard marble runs to ESP32 handhelds, Instructables publishes free step-by-step photo guides from a maker community that has been running since 2005. No account needed to read. Here is what the official pages confirm and what our quick test found.

Quick facts

  • What: Instructables, a community website of step-by-step DIY project guides with photos
  • Library: 135,000+ projects (the count shown on its Projects page) across Circuits, Workshop, Design, Craft, Cooking, Living, Outside and Teachers
  • Cost: free to read without an account; a free account adds publishing, comments and saving; no paid plan is advertised on the current site
  • Owner: Autodesk, part of the company since 2011; the site started in 2005 at Squid Labs
  • Sign-in: not needed to read full projects, our test confirms
  • Licence: each project shows its own licence badge picked by the author, so check before reusing
  • Works in India: yes, global site with no region limits indicated

What it is

Instructables is a website where makers publish step-by-step guides for things they built: electronics, woodworking, 3D printing, crafts, cooking, costumes and more. According to its About page, it started in 2005 at Squid Labs as a documentation system for open-source kitesurfing projects and became part of Autodesk in 2011. Today it describes itself as home to hundreds of thousands of projects from a worldwide community.

Besides browsing, the site runs regular contests with prizes for new projects, and a Teachers section that organises projects for classroom use. Autodesk's own free design apps, Tinkercad and Fusion, are linked from the Projects page for readers who want to design parts before building.

What we tested

On 22 October 2026 we opened instructables.com in a desktop browser with no account. This is exactly what we did and saw:

  • The Projects page loaded without a sign-in request and listed 135K projects across eight category tabs, with newest projects first.
  • We opened a featured electronics project, "Thingtendo - a Game & Watch Clone Using ESP32 S3", published 30 September 2026. All 8 steps, the photos, the supplies list and a downloadable parts-list attachment were fully visible with no sign-in wall and no payment prompt.
  • The page's "Save PDF" button produced no download when clicked while logged out. The site does not say on the page whether it needs an account, so treat one-click PDF saving as unconfirmed.
  • In our logged-out visit the sidebar carried the site's own contest promotion card rather than third-party ads. We only checked a few pages, so this may differ elsewhere.

Is it free?

Reading is free and, in our test, needed no account at all. A free account (email sign-up) adds the ability to publish your own projects, comment, follow authors and enter contests. We found no paid membership tier advertised anywhere on the current site; an old "Pro" membership mentioned in older articles is no longer promoted. If that changes, the cost to watch for would be around a few dollars a month, but as of our check there is nothing to pay.

What you can use it for

  • Learning by building: beginner-friendly projects with a photo for every step, from soldering a first circuit to baking bread
  • School and clubs: the Teachers section groups projects suitable for classrooms and workshops
  • Repair and reuse ideas: many projects are built around recycled cardboard, scrap wood or old electronics
  • Sharing your own builds: publishing is free, and featured projects can win prizes in contests

One thing to check before reusing a project's content elsewhere: the author picks a licence for each project, shown as a badge on the page. The project we opened carried a CC BY-NC 4.0 badge, which allows reuse with credit but not for commercial purposes. Badges differ from project to project.

Privacy and data

The site footer links to the Autodesk Privacy Statement, effective 1 January 2026, which covers Autodesk's websites including Instructables. It says Autodesk collects details you give it (such as your name and email when you register), collects usage data automatically through cookies and similar technologies, and that third-party sites it links to follow their own policies. You can browse projects without giving any personal data, since reading needs no account. We did not audit the company's servers or data handling; this section only summarises the published policy.

India

Instructables is a global English-language site and we found no region restrictions; it opened normally in our test. There is no India-specific pricing because no paid tier is advertised. Parts lists in electronics projects often link to international stores, so Indian readers may need to find local equivalents for components.

Good to know

  • Quality varies by author. Featured projects are reviewed by editors and tend to be the most reliable starting point.
  • Some projects need special tools such as a 3D printer, laser cutter or soldering iron. The supplies list at the top tells you before you start.
  • The comment section under each project often has fixes and answers from people who already built it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account to read projects?
No. In our test, complete projects with all steps and photos opened without signing in.

Is Instructables really free?
Yes for readers. Publishing and commenting need a free account, and we found no paid plan advertised on the current site.

Who owns Instructables?
Autodesk, the design software company, which acquired it in 2011 according to the site's About page.

Can I reuse project photos and text?
Check the licence badge on each project page. Many use Creative Commons licences such as CC BY-NC, which allow reuse with credit but not for commercial purposes.
