GeoGebra: free graphing calculator and maths apps for students

By AiCoN | Published 15 October 2026 | Sources checked 15 October 2026

GeoGebra says its graphing, geometry and calculus apps are free for non-commercial use, including students and teachers, on phones, tablets and computers, with no account needed.

What it is

GeoGebra is maths software that puts graphing, geometry, algebra, spreadsheets, statistics and calculus in one place. GeoGebra says it is "dynamic mathematics software for all levels of education" and that its online platform has over 1 million free classroom resources made by its community. It is not an AI tool, although it also lists a Math Solver that works from a photo of homework. We did not look at that feature in detail. We included GeoGebra as a free learning tool.

Is it free?

Yes, for non-commercial use. GeoGebra's download page says: "GeoGebra apps are free for non-commercial use, including use by teachers and students for learning and teaching at school, university, or home." Its licence page says commercial use needs a separate licence from GeoGebra, and gives examples such as selling courses or materials made with it, or paid training that uses it. If you are unsure whether your use is commercial, GeoGebra asks you to contact it.

What you can do with it

  • Calculator Suite: the app GeoGebra recommends for most students and teachers. It lists 2D and 3D graphing, geometry, a spreadsheet, symbolic calculation and probability in one app.
  • Phones, tablets and computers: GeoGebra says it works on all devices and can also be installed as a web app (PWA) from the browser.
  • Work offline: after you install an app, GeoGebra says the main maths tools work without internet. Saving and sharing online, sign-in and searching resources need internet.
  • No account needed: the download page says you can use the apps without an account and save work on your device.
  • Classroom resources: over a million free activities, per GeoGebra, and a Classroom tool where teachers can follow student progress.
  • Exam mode: GeoGebra says it offers exam modes that restrict internet and other apps, for tests that allow it.

What to watch out for

  • Free means non-commercial. Tutors who charge fees or anyone selling material made with it should read the licence page first.
  • Calculator Suite and GeoGebra Classic are not identical, per GeoGebra. Classic has extra tools for advanced users and authors.
  • Whether your school or exam board allows GeoGebra in an exam is a separate question. Check the rules for your exam.

Privacy and data

  • The privacy policy says you do not need to register or sign in to use the software and resources, and that GeoGebra never shares users' personal information with third parties.
  • Registering needs a username and an email address. Other profile details are optional.
  • Its website uses cookies, Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager and Google reCAPTCHA. With your consent it says it may show ads. A consent manager (Quantcast Inmobi) handles that choice.
  • Student work is saved on GeoGebra only when an account has been created.
  • The policy says it complies with the US children's privacy law COPPA, and that it does not collect personal data without the permission of a parent or school.

India

  • GeoGebra says the website is intended for visitors from all countries. We did not test it from India.

Frequently asked questions

Is GeoGebra free for students?

Yes. GeoGebra says its apps are free for non-commercial use, including learning at school, university or home.

Do I need an account?

No, per the download page. An account is only for saving work online.

Does it work offline?

Yes, after install, for the main maths tools, per GeoGebra.
