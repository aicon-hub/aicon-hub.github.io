Ando: agent messaging, waitlist and pricing boundaries

By AiCoN | Published 7 October 2026 | Sources checked 18 November 2026

Ando is a team messaging platform for humans and AI agents to share channels, threads and conversations. Access is by request. Its current pricing page lists Free, Pro and Business plans with separate included credits for built-in agents. Unlimited agent seats do not mean unlimited model usage or third-party services.

18 November 2026 update

Ando now publishes plan prices and built-in-agent credit limits. The earlier statement that no public rates were listed is out of date. Access remains by request; no workspace, payment or migration was checked by us.

What the platform changes

The launch describes agents with their own identity, permissions and shared context, rather than only a bot summoned for a single reply.

An agent participating in a channel still needs a clear purpose, allowed sources and action limits. Being a member does not authorise every message, file or decision.

Bring existing agents

The release calls Ando agent-agnostic and names several outside agents. The FAQ says developer interfaces, including MCP, support external agents.

It also warns that connecting tools does not keep a local runtime awake. The agent needs a way to receive work and remain available. Messaging integration and reliable execution are separate requirements.

Live conversations and Jams

The release describes shared conversations where agents and people can work together. This is intended to support ongoing team work rather than only isolated question-and-answer sessions.

A proactive contribution can still be wrong, distracting or outside the channel's audience. Define when an agent should speak and when it should wait for a person.

Access is by request

The current FAQ says to request access and wait for the team to reach out. It also describes a Bridge from an existing workspace and a fast-track route for some new companies. Neither is a promise that every reader can open a workspace immediately.

No access request, workspace invitation or agent connection was made for this guide. Check accepted access and onboarding terms before planning a migration.

Published plans and credit limits

The official pricing page now lists Free, Pro and Business plans in US dollars. Free is listed at $0 with no card needed, 500 credits a month shared by the workspace, 90 days of message history and up to 10 integrations.

Pro is $7.25 per person per month billed yearly, or $8.75 billed monthly. It includes 500 credits a month per person, pooled by the team, unlimited message history and unlimited integrations.

Business is $15 per person per month billed yearly, or $18 billed monthly. It includes 1,200 credits a month per person instead of Pro's 500, plus listed controls including agent permission scopes, an audit trail, SSO/SCIM and retention controls.

All plans allow unlimited agent identities without charging them a human seat. Built-in Ando agents still use credits; extra credits can be bought, including on Free. No tax-inclusive Indian price, currency conversion or checkout was verified. New workspaces are advertised with a seven-day trial; confirm the chosen plan and billing before accepting any paid terms.

External agents have separate costs

The pricing page says agents you connect yourself run on your own subscription and do not use Ando credits. Their model, hosting and service costs can still apply. An unlimited number of agent identities is not a promise of free compute.

Check the actual onboarding offer, credit top-up prices, renewal terms and who pays each provider. No purchase or account test was performed.

Slack migration is not everything

The FAQ recommends a pilot and reviewing Slack sync documentation for supported conversations and migration limits. It says not to assume every channel or direct message is imported.

Check source permissions and the intended audience before copying conversation history. A sync feature does not create consent to widen the audience of an old private discussion.

A small team pilot

  1. Confirm accepted access and actual onboarding terms.
  2. Choose a limited project and approved participants.
  3. List the content the workspace may receive.
  4. Check roles, agent identities and access controls.
  5. Connect only the required external agent capabilities.
  6. Verify runtime availability and error handling.
  7. Test a private-content denial and a wrong-action request.
  8. Review logs, messages and costs.
  9. Expand only after the pilot meets the team's needs.

No actual workspace or migration was checked by us.

Funding and product proof

The company-issued release announces twenty million dollars in seed funding. Funding is a company event, not a product-quality test, service-level guarantee or customer licence.

The launch's claims about adoption and collaboration should remain attributed to the company. We did not independently audit team use.

India and data questions

No Indian hosting region, tax-inclusive local tariff, regional checkout or Malayalam performance was verified. Ask about retention, exports, subprocessors and account closure for the proposed workspace.

People remain responsible for judgment and important decisions, as the FAQ itself says. Keep approvals for external communications and consequential changes.

What changed since the original post?

The 18 November check replaces the earlier no-public-rate description with published plan prices and credit allowances. Access remains by request. The runtime, migration and permission cautions still apply.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a public final price?

Yes. The current page lists US-dollar Free, Pro and Business plans. Included credits and billing cadence matter; no tax-inclusive Indian checkout price was checked.

Does tool connection keep my local agent running?

The FAQ says no.

Was a workspace created?

No.
