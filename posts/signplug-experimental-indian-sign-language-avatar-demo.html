SignPlug: try an experimental Indian Sign Language avatar demo

By AiCoN | Checked 18 November 2026 | IIIT-H announcement: 17 November 2026

SignPlug turns English sentences into an animated Indian Sign Language (ISL) avatar in a browser. It is a research project from IIIT Hyderabad and the University of Bath, with a public demo you can explore. It is not a qualified interpreter, a sign-language course or a tool to rely on for emergencies.

What is useful about this demo?

Instead of returning only written text, it displays a moving 3D avatar with a timeline, replay and highlighted sign labels. It offers a concrete look at research on making web content available in ISL. Treat it as an experiment to explore, not proof that a sentence has been translated correctly.

Try the public Sign Chat

  1. Open the research project page, choose Live Synthesis Demo, then ISL Sign Chat. If the embedded panel stays blank, use its Open in a new tab link.
  2. The direct university-hosted route is ISL Sign Chat. Allow the avatar and sentence library to load.
  3. Pick a sample sentence or type a short, non-sensitive English sentence, then press Sign.
  4. Watch the avatar and highlighted labels. Use replay or the timeline to inspect the animation. A word without a stored sign may be spelled letter by letter instead.

What happened in our test?

On 18 November, we typed I need water into the public university demo. It returned the labels I / WATER / NEED, marked NEED as fingerspelled, and generated a 169-frame sequence. We observed avatar playback and the timeline. The demo also loaded a library labelled 100 sentences.

This confirms generation and playback for that test, not ISL accuracy or understandability. We did not have a qualified ISL signer assess the result. We did not test Malayalam input, phone usability, a full website integration or every sample sentence.

The research limits matter

  • The current avatar lacks facial expressions and other non-manual markers that carry grammar and meaning in ISL. The researchers also identify context-dependent sign forms as future work.
  • The project reports a study with 11 Deaf and hard-of-hearing ISL signers watching 34 avatar sentences. Its 88.8% result is exact identification in a four-option task, not a general translation-accuracy score.
  • The same study reports average ratings of 2.67/5 for understandability, 2.61/5 for hand shape and 3.0/5 for web-demo usability. These results should not be hidden behind the larger percentage.
  • Do not use the demo as a substitute for qualified interpretation, a trusted ISL teaching resource, or accessible communication that a Deaf person has agreed works for them. Avoid using it for medical, legal, financial or emergency communication.

Cost and privacy

No login, payment or card was requested in our public demo test. We did not establish a long-term free plan, usage allowance, service guarantee or data-retention policy. Use only harmless example text, not names, identifiers, health information or private conversations.

The project describes browser-side avatar animation. It also describes an English-to-gloss translation stage using an LLM. Do not assume the entire text-to-sign workflow stays on your device just because playback runs in the browser.

What AiCoN checked

We read IIIT-H's announcement, the linked research project, its study results and source-code repository. We opened the project demo, followed its university-hosted Sign Chat route and ran the short test above. The embedded panel initially appeared blank; the direct Sign Chat route worked. No account was created and no personal information was entered. This is a limited experimental-demo introduction, not a product recommendation or linguistic review.
