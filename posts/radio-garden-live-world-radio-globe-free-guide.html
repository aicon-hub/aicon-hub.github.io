Radio Garden: spin a globe and tune into live radio from any city, free

By AiCoN | Published 22 October 2026 | Sources checked 22 October 2026

Spin a satellite globe, rest the crosshair on any green dot, and a live local radio station from that city starts playing. Free, no signup, works beautifully from India. Here is what the official pages confirm, what our hands-on test found, and the ads to expect.

Quick facts

  • What: Radio Garden, a free website (and free mobile app) that puts live local radio stations from around the world on a 3D globe you spin and tap
  • Cost: completely free, no paid tier; the service is supported by advertising
  • Signup: none needed on the website; stations play straight away
  • Ads: yes. We saw Google ad banners, and during our session a full-screen interstitial ad covered the whole page once
  • Made by: Radio Garden BV, a company in the Netherlands (site footer, checked 22 October 2026)
  • Runs on: any modern browser; free apps on Google Play and the App Store
  • Works in India: yes. From India the globe opened centred over Lucknow, and Indian stations played (details below)

What it is

Radio Garden turns the Earth into a radio dial. You see a satellite globe covered in small green dots, and every dot is a live radio station. Drag to spin the globe, rest the crosshair on a dot, and that station starts playing, with the city name, country and even the local time shown. There is nothing to install and no account: the website just plays.

Beyond the globe, the site has a Browse mode, a Search box, Favourites you mark with a heart (and can move between devices with a transfer code), a "Lock station" toggle that keeps your station while you spin, and a "Balloon Ride" button that lifts you off to a surprise station somewhere else on Earth. The settings panel offers dark mode, increased contrast, globe dot size and a long list of languages.

What we tested

On 22 October 2026 we opened radio.garden in a desktop browser with no account. This is exactly what we did and saw:

  • The entry page invited us to "Explore live radio by rotating the globe". One click on "Start Radio Garden" loaded the globe, already centred over India with station dots across the country.
  • We picked Lucknow. A drawer listed "Stations in Lucknow" and picks from the area: BBD FM 90.8 (Lucknow), Radio Gyanasthali 89.6 FM (Gonda), Tikapur FM 101, Radio Madhyapaschim FM 91.4 (Tulsipur), Radio Aashiqanaa (Kanpur) and The JP Daily 96.3 FM (Chainpur).
  • We pressed play on BBD FM 90.8. The player bar switched to the playing state with a stop button, the station row lit up green and the map crosshair glowed. One honest caveat: our test browser runs without speakers, so we verified the playing state on screen but could not hear the audio ourselves.
  • We opened the settings panel: dark mode, contrast and language options are all there, along with the privacy policy and a transfer code for moving favourites between devices.

Is it free?

Yes, entirely. There is no subscription and nothing to unlock. The service earns from advertising, and you will notice it: Google ad banners sit on the entry page and below the player, and once during our visit a full-screen interstitial ad (with its own close button) appeared over the whole page. The radio itself never stopped being free, but do not expect an ad-free experience.

What you can use it for

  • Discovering your own region: smaller Indian cities have FM and community stations on the map that you may never find in a search box
  • Language and culture: hear how a city actually sounds, from Tokyo pop to Chennai talk radio, in one spin
  • Background listening: lock a station you like, then keep browsing the globe without losing it
  • Travel nostalgia: revisit the stations of a city you lived in or visited

Privacy and data

According to Radio Garden's published privacy policy (effective 1 July 2019), the service may collect an email address if you give one, usage data such as your IP address, browser type and pages visited, and location data if you allow it. It uses session, preference, security and advertising cookies, and uses Google Analytics and Firebase for statistics. You can read the policy yourself under Settings, Privacy Policy. We did not audit Radio Garden's servers or network traffic; this section summarises the provider's own published policy and what we observed on the page, which included Google advertising cookies.

India

Works well from India. In our test the globe opened directly over India, Lucknow's station drawer listed six stations from the region, and a "Popular in India" shelf surfaced stations such as Vaigai FM (Rajapalayam) and KodaicityFM (Kodaikanal). The interface is English by default with more languages in settings. Streaming quality depends on each station's own broadcast stream, so a slow connection affects some stations more than others.

Good to know

  • Radio Garden is a player and directory: the audio comes from each broadcaster's own stream, so a station can occasionally be offline or move.
  • The full-screen interstitial ad can be startling; it has a small Close button at the top right.
  • The free Android and iPhone apps are linked right on the settings page if you prefer listening on your phone.
  • Favourites live on your device; use the transfer code in settings to carry them to another device.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account?
No. The website plays stations with no signup. Favourites are saved on your device and can be moved with a transfer code.

Is it really free?
Yes. There is no paid tier; the service is ad-supported, including banners and an occasional full-screen ad.

Can I request a missing station?
Yes. The settings page has a "Submit a Radio Station" link for broadcasters and listeners to suggest stations.

Who makes it?
Radio Garden BV, a company in the Netherlands, according to the copyright line in the site's settings page.
