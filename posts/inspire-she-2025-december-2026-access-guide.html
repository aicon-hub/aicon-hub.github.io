GOVERNMENT SCIENCE SCHOLARSHIP

INSPIRE-SHE 2025: eligibility, subjects, benefits and how to apply by 7 December 2026

By AiCoN | Published 18 November 2026 | Updated and official sources checked 18 November 2026

Applications for the Department of Science and Technology's INSPIRE Scholarship for Higher Education, SHE-2025 batch, are open from 7 November to 7 December 2026, 23:59 IST. The current batch FAQ is for students who passed Class XII in 2025 and entered an eligible basic or natural science degree in the same year. This is not a fresh-entry scholarship for every current BSc or MSc student.

The money, correctly separated: the scholarship is Rs60,000 per year, subject to selection and continuation rules. For projects in India, Rs20,000 mentorship support is available only in scholarship years 3 and 4, not every year. A separate conditional fourth-year overseas-project option exists. An own-name, Aadhaar-linked SBI savings account is required.

Which official rules does this guide use?

This guide uses the SHE-2025 FAQ linked on the current portal and the post-offer guidelines effective from the 2025 batch onwards. These differ materially from older general FAQs on subjects, mentorship funding, selection and continuation. Use the batch-specific documents, your offer letter and later official notices, not an older year's terms.

The homepage confirms the application window. Its separate SHE-2025 call-document link returned to the homepage during our check. An exact batch date-of-birth range and numeric allocations for individual boards were not independently established from that call. The current FAQ does establish the 2025 school-leaving batch, general age group, 28 undergraduate subjects and board-quota formula below.

What is INSPIRE-SHE?

SHE is a Government of India scholarship under DST's Innovation in Science Pursuit for Inspired Research programme. Its purpose is to encourage students to study basic and natural sciences and pursue research. The current documents provide for 12,000 scholarships, distributed through board-wise quotas and selection priorities. Being eligible to apply does not guarantee an award.

Who can apply for the 2025 batch?

  • School and degree year: the current FAQ specifies Class XII pass-outs of 2025 who enrolled in the eligible degree in that same year.
  • Age: the FAQ gives 17-22 years. It does not supply a date-of-birth interval in the sections we could verify. Check the current application and call for the precise date calculation rather than assuming a cutoff.
  • Degree: BSc, BS, four-year BSc with Research, Integrated MSc or Integrated MS in supported basic and natural sciences. The post-offer rules also refer to BSc General, BSc Honours and BS-MS programmes. The institution must be a recognised college, university or academic institution in India.
  • Eligibility route: satisfy an applicable route below, as well as the course, subject and batch conditions.

Routes stated in the current FAQ

  1. Board top 1%: aggregate Class XII marks within the top 1% of the relevant State or Central Education Board. This is not a universal requirement to score 99% marks.
  2. Entrance-rank route: rank within the top 10,000 in IIT-JEE Main or Advanced, or NEET, while pursuing an eligible basic or natural science degree in India. A rank does not make an engineering or medical degree eligible.
  3. Talent route: NTSE scholars, JBNSTS scholars and International Olympiad medallists pursuing the specified basic and natural science courses.

The FAQ's talent-route paragraph refers to Bachelor/Master-level study, but its batch-entry rule still specifies 2025 Class XII completion and same-year degree entry. Do not read that paragraph as a general invitation for any existing MSc student. If your situation is unusual, obtain official clarification before relying on eligibility.

Courses the current FAQ excludes

Engineering, Medicine, Military Science, Defence Studies, Agriculture, Psychology, Seed Technology, Home Science, Geography, Economics, Education including BSc-BEd dual degrees, Computer Science, Computer Applications, Instrumentation, Information Technology, Physical Education, distance education at open universities and other professional courses are outside its supported list. A supported subject name does not by itself turn a professional degree into an eligible science degree.

How selection and board quotas work

The 2025 documents allocate the 12,000 scholarships in proportion to the number of students who appeared in Class XII examinations of each State/Central Board:

Board quota = (students appearing in that board's Class XII examination / total students appearing across boards) x 12,000.

Within the respective board quota, the stated preference order is:

  1. Board-top-1% students enrolled in Integrated MSc and BS-MS programmes.
  2. Students meeting the specified JEE/NEET rank or NTSE/JBNSTS/International Olympiad route and enrolled in supported BSc/BS/integrated science courses.
  3. Board-top-1% students enrolled in BSc General, BSc Honours or BS.

This is a competitive allocation, not an automatic payment to everyone in the top 1%. We have not confirmed numeric 2025 quotas for Kerala, CBSE, CISCE or other individual boards. Use official batch allocations when issued. The formula in this batch's rules uses students appearing for Class XII, not the older general FAQ's valid-application formula.

Scholarship amount, duration and first payment

  • Annual scholarship: Rs60,000, expressed as Rs5,000 per month for calculation. This does not promise a monthly bank credit.
  • Maximum duration: five years or course/MSc completion, whichever is earlier, provided all continuation conditions are met.
  • First release: first- and second-year scholarships are paid together in the second year after approval of the required first-year marksheet and performance report and continuation in the course. The two-year scholarship total is Rs1,20,000, not an advance paid on registration.
  • Later instalments: depend on approval of the previous year's academic documents and any required MSc enrolment proof.

The FAQ describes selection as taking at least four months on average after applications close. It gives a usual release period of 1-2 months after relevant documents are approved. These are guidance, not guaranteed payment dates. If unpaid even after two months of approval, the FAQ directs scholars to inspire.prog-dst@nic.in.

Mentorship projects: India and overseas options

Projects in India

The funded mentorship years are scholarship years 3 and 4 only, Rs20,000 in each year. No mentorship grant is admissible in scholarship years 1, 2 or 5. The project must run for 6-8 weeks at an institute, university or laboratory in India outside the parent institution. Obtain prior permission from the competent authority of your college/university, such as the Principal, Head or Dean as specified in the documents.

To claim the grant, upload the prescribed Project Completion Certificate and Research Project Report, signed by the host institution's competent authority and the supervisor/mentor, together with the required home-institution permission. Upload within three months of completing the project. Group projects are not accepted. The grant is not an unconditional annual cash bonus.

Conditional fourth-year project abroad

The current rules allow a fourth-year scholar to carry out a research mentorship project outside India with a maximum Rs1 lakh lump-sum grant, subject to a condition concerning unclaimed third- and fourth-year mentorship grants or absence of other domestic/overseas agency support. The wording linking those conditions is not fully clear; obtain written DST confirmation for your funding situation. Do not assume this is Rs1 lakh added to the Indian-project grants.

Prior Principal permission is required. The post-offer rules list the prescribed completion certificate, project report, permission and travel bills including airfare tickets and boarding passes. Project documents must be uploaded within three months of completion. Confirm approval and exact claim eligibility with DST before committing travel expenses.

Application documents to prepare

  • Class XII marksheet.
  • Class X marksheet/certificate as date-of-birth proof.
  • The prescribed endorsement certificate signed by the college Principal, institute Director or university Registrar. An informal letter or a department-level signature is not a substitute.
  • Rank/award certificate for JEE Main/Advanced, NEET, JBNSTS, NTSE or International Olympiad, if using that eligibility route.
  • Board eligibility/advisory note, if provided. The FAQ marks this optional.

Use the current portal's prescribed formats and current photo/file specifications. Incomplete applications can be rejected. Do not infer fresh-application upload limits from the separate post-offer document-upload guide.

How to apply online

  1. Go to the official INSPIRE portal. Confirm SHE-2025 and the 7 December 2026, 23:59 IST closing time.
  2. Read the current SHE FAQ and relevant guidelines. Use Scholarship, not Fellowship or Faculty, which are separate programmes.
  3. For a new account, choose New User? Register here. The public registration form asks for name, mobile number, date of birth, email, password, scheme, identification details and captcha. Use your own accurate details and an email you can keep accessing.
  4. Complete registration and log in through the official portal. Follow the active scholarship application screens for personal, education, course and eligibility information.
  5. Obtain the prescribed institutional endorsement and upload the documents required for your route. Check board marks, school-leaving year, degree entry year and exact subject carefully.
  6. Review the full preview and submit before the closing time. Save the submitted application/acknowledgement and your reference details. Do not assume that saving a draft completes submission.
  7. Monitor the portal for selection, clarification requests and document deadlines. If selected, download the provisional offer and comply with its conditions.

Our access check stopped at the blank public registration form. We did not create an applicant account, enter personal information, upload documents or submit an application. The authenticated screens, final submission, edit/withdrawal route and individual selection were not checked by us. These steps describe the official route, not a claim that an application was completed.

SBI account requirement and six-month deadline after offer

The FAQ requires a savings account in the scholar's own name at State Bank of India only, linked with Aadhaar. Joint accounts and other banks are not accepted. The 2025 post-offer rules specifically require a new SBI savings account with no transaction limit.

Upload a self-attested copy of the passbook's front page and Aadhaar/Aadhaar enrolment slip within six months of the provisional offer letter. The FAQ also says to sign the passbook page before uploading. Failure to provide account details in time cancels the provisional offer and disables further document uploads.

The guidelines warn that invalid, inactive, dormant, joint, minor, limited, no-frills/zero-balance and Jan Dhan accounts can be returned for correction. Act on an account-related portal notification within one month. Check the exact SBI product with the bank and DST rather than assuming every SBI account qualifies. Upload bank/identity documents only through the official account, not to a helper in chat.

Selection is provisional: what happens next?

Selected students receive a provisional offer in their INSPIRE portal account; selection/rejection lists and letters are also made available through the portal. Download and read the offer. Confirmation depends on the eligible subjects/course and required first-year academic documents. A provisional offer is not a guarantee of money regardless of later performance.

Use the INSPIRE/SHE registration number stated in the offer when corresponding with DST, not your university registration number. Follow the offer's specific document deadline. The post-offer rules do not treat registration alone as confirmation of an award.

Strict academic continuation rules for the 2025 batch

  • Pass all subjects in the first attempt and obtain at least 60% where results are expressed in percentage, or 7.0 CGPA on a 10-point scale or equivalent where results use CGPA, for the annual examination or two semesters.
  • The rules do not allow conversion between CGPA and percentage to meet this requirement.
  • Failure or a below-threshold result in the first year makes the scholar ineligible even for the first-year scholarship and for the entire SHE period.
  • Failure or a below-threshold result in later years stops the scholarship immediately and makes the student ineligible for later scholarship and mentorship instalments. Missing the annual/semester examination also triggers discontinuation under the FAQ.
  • A break/gap year during the programme disqualifies the scholar and ends mentorship support. Discontinuing the course withdraws the offer even if the student rejoins later.

For BSc Honours, clause 7(c) specifies passing all subjects in the first attempt, with at least 60% or 7.0 CGPA/equivalent in the mains/major Honours subjects. Read that clause alongside the general continuation rule and seek DST clarification if your marksheet mixes major/minor results or uses another grading scheme. Do not assume the older rule of losing one year and automatically resuming applies to this batch.

Annual documents and payment workflow

Upload the prescribed annual Performance Report and marksheet together after results. For semester courses, include both semesters for the academic year, using the portal's required combined file. The Performance Report must recommend continuation/discontinuation and carry an authorised institutional signature.

The FAQ identifies Vice-Chancellor/Registrar/Dean of Science for universities, and Principal/Vice-Principal/Proctor/Officer-in-Charge for colleges. It says signatures from other officers such as a Head of Department, section officer or student-welfare officer are not accepted for this purpose.

Use self-attested academic marksheets. Provisional marksheets are not accepted. If originals are unavailable, the rules allow an internet marksheet attested by the Controller of Examinations or Registrar. Prefer uploading within two months of the yearly result. The rules set a maximum 12-month submission period for a completed academic year ending in June; follow your batch/offer schedule. Two consecutive academic years without the annual report and marksheet lead to withdrawal.

The separate post-offer upload guide lists the SBI passbook front page, Performance Report and academic marksheets as separate PDF uploads, maximum 1 MB each. It describes logging in, opening Scholarship and Related Link, viewing the offer, entering bank details and using Documents for BSc/MSc for yearly uploads. These are post-offer instructions, not proof of the current fresh-application form layout. Use the portal's current validation if a screen differs. The guide says not to send documents by post.

MSc continuation and all 35 postgraduate subjects

An existing scholar can continue from a three-year BSc into a two-year MSc, or from a four-year BSc Research/BS route into a one-year MSc, within the overall five-year limit, in supported sciences. Submit the prescribed MSc enrolment proof. Integrated students follow their course's year-wise requirements. MTech is not supported; a gap before MSc breaks continuation.

Changing a course or college, other support and later fellowship

The implementation rules permit a subject/institution change during the first BSc year only within the identified subjects. A transfer between colleges affiliated to the same university is permitted during the BSc tenure within supported subjects. Other changes can end the scholarship without restoration. Obtain official confirmation before moving.

The older general DST FAQ prohibits concurrent scholarship funding, but the current 2025 FAQ does not repeat that question. Declare other support and check the current offer/DST's written instructions before accepting overlapping awards; do not assume the omission grants permission.

A scholar who withdraws SHE after BSc and later applies for INSPIRE Fellowship is considered only under the general-candidate route and must satisfy that programme's own first-rank/qualification conditions. SHE is not an automatic PhD fellowship or a guarantee of later admission.

Final checklist before submitting

  • 2025 Class XII completion and same-year entry into a supported degree.
  • Applicable age and board/rank/talent route, exact subject and institutional eligibility.
  • Correct marksheets, prescribed endorsement and route certificate.
  • Application submitted, not merely saved, by 7 December 2026, 23:59 IST.
  • Understanding of SBI account rules, provisional selection, academic thresholds and document deadlines.
  • No assumption that mentorship is paid every year or that selection is guaranteed.

Official sources and help

  • INSPIRE portal: live SHE-2025 window, registration, notices and formats.
  • Current SHE-2025 FAQ: batch eligibility, 28 UG subjects, benefits, bank, selection and continuation.
  • Post-offer implementation guidelines, effective 2025 onwards: board quotas, priorities, 35 PG subjects, payments, academic rules and mentorship claims.
  • Post-offer account and annual-document upload guide.
  • Older general DST FAQ: background only where it does not conflict with current batch rules.

For questions about your eligibility, course mapping, offer or payment, use the official portal's Contact Us/support route. The current FAQ gives inspire.prog-dst@nic.in for unresolved payment queries after its stated waiting period. Include your INSPIRE/SHE reference when applicable, but never send passwords or OTPs.
