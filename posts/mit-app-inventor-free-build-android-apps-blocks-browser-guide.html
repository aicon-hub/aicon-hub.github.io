MIT App Inventor: free app building with blocks in your browser

By AiCoN | Published 17 October 2026 | Sources checked 17 October 2026

MIT App Inventor says it is free and runs in your browser, with nothing to install. You build apps from blocks and test them on your phone. A Google account is needed.

What it is

MIT App Inventor is a tool for building Android apps by snapping together blocks, run by MIT. Its home page says "App Inventor is free and runs right in your browser on a Mac, Windows computer, or Chromebook. There's nothing to install." You can test your app on your own phone with a free companion app. It is aimed at learners, but it also has tutorials that use AI, such as an image classifier and an app that describes a photo aloud. We included it as a free way to learn by making something real.

Is it free?

Yes, per the home page: "App Inventor is free", and the MIT AI2 Companion app for iPhone or Android is described as free. We saw no price or plan. We did not check whether the AI tutorials use any outside service with limits or costs.

What you can do with it

  • Start easy. The home page lists beginner projects of about an hour each, such as "Hello Purr" and "Talk to Me".
  • Try AI. Intermediate and advanced tutorials include "GenAI Digital Doodle", "GenAI Vision Aid" and "PICaboo", where you train an image classifier.
  • Test on a phone. Connect your own phone or tablet with the free MIT AI2 Companion app, per the home page.
  • Step-by-step guides. The home page says each tutorial opens a working project with instructions beside it.

What to watch out for

  • Google account needed. The terms say you need a Google account to log in to MIT App Inventor.
  • Android apps. The terms say it lets you develop applications for Android devices. The home page says you can test on iPhone through the companion app, but we did not check what that allows.
  • Adjacent learning tools. If you want a simpler start for young learners, Scratch is a different, block-based option we covered earlier.
  • Not tested. We did not log in or build anything.

Privacy and data

  • The terms and privacy policy (modified 1 July 2020) say MIT only has access to your Google account name and email address.
  • They say MIT tracks which pages are visited, and also information about user projects and how they are developed when connected to Android devices.
  • They say MIT may log your IP address, operating system and browser, and use cookies and web analysis tools.
  • They say information may be used to debug and improve the system and for scientific research on how people learn and create, and that published findings are non-personally identifiable.
  • The terms say the site runs on Google's App Engine service.

India

  • The home page says it is used in 200 countries and regions. It mentions no country limits. We did not test it from India.
