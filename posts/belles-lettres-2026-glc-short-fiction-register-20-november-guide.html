Belles-Lettres 2026: register by 20 November for GLC Mumbai's short-fiction competition

By Muhammed Salman | Published 19 November 2026 | Registration: Friday, 20 November | Story submission: Saturday, 5 December

The Magazine Committee of Government Law College, Mumbai is inviting individual short-fiction entries for the J. E. Dastur Memorial Belles-Lettres competition 2026. Students enrolled in a graduation course in any field, and postgraduate diploma students, can explore the contest. The organiser announces no registration fee and a total prize pool of Rs25,000, with a first prize of Rs10,000.

Two different deadlines, and a strict no-AI-writing rule.

Register by 20 November 2026, then submit your story by 5 December 2026. The sources reviewed do not give a clock time or timezone for either cutoff, so do not wait until late on those dates. The brochure says using artificial intelligence to write or rephrase words results in disqualification. This guide explains the process; it does not provide a story or rewrite one for entry.

Who can participate?

  • You must have passed HSC, CBSE, ISC Class 12 or an equivalent examination.
  • You must currently be enrolled in a graduation course, in any field. This is not restricted to law students.
  • Postgraduate diploma students are eligible, but postgraduate degree students are not.
  • Only single authorship is permitted. Co-authored entries are not allowed.

The organising committee has the final decision on eligibility. If your programme does not fit these labels clearly, ask before registering rather than assuming that a form accepting your details establishes eligibility. The organiser's current announcement describes the competition as national; it does not make the award automatic for an eligible student.

Prizes and fee

  • First prize: Rs10,000.
  • Second prize: Rs7,000.
  • Third prize: Rs5,000.
  • Fourth and fifth prizes: Rs1,500 each.

These five awards total Rs25,000. They are competitive prizes, not a payment for every submission. The Magazine Committee's 4 November 2026 announcement explicitly says there is no registration fee. We have not verified a payment date, tax treatment or a guaranteed publication benefit, so none is promised here.

Choose one of four story prompts

The brochure gives four alternatives. Choose only one, not all four:

  1. A prescribed opening line about an ancient magical time when tigers smoked. Copy the exact sentence from the original brochure, including its wording and punctuation.
  2. A story incorporating a taxidermied goat, a purse of pearls and an opened envelope.
  3. A story built around the three named characters in the brochure: Confucious, Dr Doofenshmirtz and Mulan. Check the source's exact names when choosing this option.
  4. A story based on the image printed on the brochure's image-prompt page. View that image in the original PDF rather than substituting a similar picture.

This guide does not reproduce or generate an entry. Write your own story and do not ask an AI tool to write or rephrase it. Originality and unpublished status are required; plagiarism leads to automatic disqualification.

Formatting and anonymous submission

The brochure requires an English story of 1,500 to 3,500 words. It warns that word-limit violations lose marks. Use Times New Roman, 12-point type, 1.5 line spacing, justified alignment, A4 paper and one-inch margins on every side.

Prepare both a legible .doc or .docx file and a PDF. The Word file must open legibly in Google Docs or Microsoft Word. Only one entry per participant is permitted. Do not put your name, college, university or other identifying information into the submission text/document; the brochure says identity disclosure can lead to rejection and disqualification.

Ask the organiser about the filename before submitting.

One brochure section says the submission document title should include the chosen topic and team code. A later competition rule says the title must be the allotted team code. Those instructions are not identical. We have not chosen one on your behalf. Confirm the required file/document title with the organiser and follow any current written clarification.

Despite individual entry, the brochure uses the label team code. It says this code will be sent after the registration deadline and asks participants not to contact the committee about the code before then. Do not mistake registering for having completed the final story submission.

Register first, submit the story later

  1. Read the original brochure and current organiser announcement linked below. Check eligibility, deadlines, all four prompts and the no-AI rule.
  2. Use the registration link embedded in the brochure. We followed it and read the current blank form titled Registration Form for Belles-Lettres 2026.
  3. The blank form asks for full name, mail ID, contact number, educational institute, year of study and a truthfulness confirmation. Check each value before submitting. No payment question was visible in that registration form.
  4. Keep your registration acknowledgement if one is issued and watch your own email for the allotted code after registration closes. We did not register, so this guide does not claim that a confirmation or code was received.
  5. Write and format your own entry. Resolve the filename conflict, remove identifying details and check both export files.
  6. Use the brochure's separate submission form by 5 December. That link required Google sign-in during our check. The signed-in upload fields and final receipt were not checked by us.

The registration links shared through the organiser announcement and the brochure, plus a different shortlink in a secondary report, all resolved to the same current registration form during this check. Start from the organiser/brochure link rather than assuming every forwarded form is genuine. Never send a password or OTP to someone offering to submit for you.

How entries are judged

The brochure allocates 25 marks each to relevance to topic, novelty and innovation, language and grammar, and plot and creativity: 100 marks total. It says submissions undergo online plagiarism checks and decisions in a dispute rest with the professor in charge and student editors. The committee reserves the right to reject non-compliant submissions without feedback.
