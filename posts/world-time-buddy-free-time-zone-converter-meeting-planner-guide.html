World Time Buddy: free time zone converter and meeting planner

By AiCoN | Published 22 October 2026 | Sources checked 22 October 2026

World Time Buddy is a free world clock and time zone converter: add cities, read their hours side by side, drag to pick a call time. No account needed. We tested an IST conversion and its daylight-saving warnings.

Quick facts

  • What it does: a world clock, time zone converter and meeting planner: add cities or time zones and see their hours side by side on one page, then drag across the hour tiles to pick a meeting time that works for everyone.
  • Price: free. We found no paid plan or upgrade page on the pages checked (the old /pro and /pricing addresses redirect to the homepage). It carries third-party advertising, per its privacy policy.
  • Account: optional. Everything we tested worked signed out; signing in (Google, Facebook or email) adds extras like renaming and grouping locations.
  • Made by: Helloka LLC, a US company, per its Terms of Service; the site's footer reads 2011-2026.
  • Best for: anyone in India working with colleagues, clients or family abroad - line up IST with their local time in seconds and never botch a call time again.

What it is

World Time Buddy shows each location as a row of hour tiles, lined up so you can read across: when it is 9:30 am in New Delhi, you can see at a glance what time it is in London or New York. You add places by searching for a city or a time zone (UTC works too), reorder rows by dragging, and select a time range by dragging across the tiles - the selection can be opened in your calendar, copied to the clipboard, or shared as a public event page. It warns you a week before a location's daylight-saving change, can highlight non-Saturday/Sunday weekends, offers 12/24-hour and mixed formats, and can overlay your Google Calendar to find open slots (optional, via Settings). There are also free clock widgets for websites, an event widget, a mobile app and a Chrome extension.

What we tested

On 22 October 2026 we ran a scripted functional test in a desktop browser, signed out. The default view showed three cities whose times matched exactly against UTC (7:45 pm in Dearborn, Michigan = 8:45 pm in Buenos Aires = 12:45 am in London). We typed "New Delhi" in the location box, picked the India suggestion, and a New Delhi IST row appeared showing 5:18 am IST - the correct conversion (UTC+5:30) at that moment, with half-hour tiles matching India's 30-minute offset. The page also carried a live daylight-saving warning: "3 days until London moves back by 1 hour (at 2:00 am, Oct 25)", which matches the real UK clock change. No account was needed for any of this.

Is it free?

Yes. We found no paid plan, checkout or feature paywall on the site; its old /pro and /pricing addresses simply redirect to the homepage. The privacy policy says the site is supported by third-party advertising (ad cookies and similar tech). Some conveniences - renaming locations, grouping them into tabs - ask you to sign in, but the account itself is free.

Privacy and data

  • No account is needed to use the converter. If you do sign up, the privacy policy says the only data point stored is your email address, that it is "never sold or disclosed to any 3rd party", and that you can request account deletion by emailing support.
  • The site uses third-party advertising companies that may collect non-personally-identifiable information (browser type, pages visited, ads clicked) via cookies, as stated in the same policy.
  • The Terms of Service date from February 2013, so the legal pages are old; the privacy policy is short and plain-English.

India

  • IST conversion worked correctly in our test, including the 30-minute offset; searching for Indian cities (New Delhi tested) works.
  • Everything is free, so there is no pricing to convert. We did not test from India itself.
  • The weekend-highlighting feature supports countries with non-Saturday/Sunday weekends.

Good to know

  • Use "Link to this view" to share your exact multi-city setup with someone.
  • The Google Calendar overlay (Settings) shows your busy slots on the grid, but needs sign-in.
  • The free embeddable clock and event widgets are handy for team pages and event invites.

Frequently asked questions

Is World Time Buddy free?

Yes, and we found no paid tier at all - the site is ad-supported, per its privacy policy.

Do I need to sign up?

No. Adding cities, converting times, dragging to select a meeting slot and sharing a link all worked signed out in our test. Sign-in only adds extras like renaming and grouping locations.

Does it handle daylight-saving changes?

Yes - it shows a warning a week before a location's clocks change; we saw the live warning for London's 25 October switch.

Can I share my city lineup?

Yes - "Link to this view" creates a shareable URL for your exact setup.
