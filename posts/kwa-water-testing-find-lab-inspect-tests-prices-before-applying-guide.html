KWA water testing: find a lab and inspect test prices before applying

By AiCoN | Published 9 November 2026 | Sources checked 9 November 2026

Kerala Water Authority (KWA) has a public form that lists its water-testing laboratories, the tests each one offers and the displayed rate for each test. You can read the options before you enter any personal details. This guide shows how, and what to ask the lab before you collect a sample or pay anything.

Quick facts

  • Where: the KWA quality-test form at qpay.kwa.kerala.gov.in/office. KWA also has a water-testing page at kwa.kerala.gov.in/en/water-testing/ with laboratory contact numbers.
  • The tests are paid services. The rates shown on the form are not a confirmed final total.
  • Checked by AiCoN on 9 November 2026: we chose Domestic, then Malappuram, then Quality Control District Laboratory Manjeri. Six laboratories loaded for the district, and a table of tests and rates appeared. We entered no personal details.
  • Rates we saw for that one lab and category: Total Coliform and E. coli (microbiological) Rs 500, pH Rs 65, turbidity Rs 80, and a row called Full Package at Rs 850. Do not assume other labs, categories or later visits show the same tests or prices.
  • A package name does not tell you which contaminants are tested. Read the parameter names in the table.

How do you look at the options?

  1. Open the KWA quality-test form and check that the address is qpay.kwa.kerala.gov.in.
  2. Choose the category. We checked Domestic only, not Commercial.
  3. Choose your district and wait for the laboratory list to load.
  4. Choose the laboratory you plan to contact and wait for the table to fill.
  5. Scroll inside the table. It scrolls on its own, so some rows are below the first screen.
  6. Read each parameter and its rate, not just the package name.

What should you ask the lab before collecting a sample?

Use the contact numbers on the KWA water-testing page and call the lab you picked. Ask which tests suit your question, what a result would and would not tell you, which container to use and whether the lab supplies it, how much water is needed, how to collect and store it, and how soon it must reach the lab.

This guide gives no sample-collection steps. Requirements can differ by test, so follow the lab and not a general tip.

Check the cost and the deadline first

The form says an extra Rs 100 is added when you pick individual parameters, up to three. We did not check how the form calculates this, or any taxes, payment charges or the final amount. Confirm the full cost with the lab.

The payment terms on the public form say that if you do not hand in the sample at the chosen lab within 15 days of applying online, the application is rejected and there is no refund in that case. That is an office deadline, not advice on how long a sample stays suitable. Ask the lab how soon your sample must arrive. We did not go to the payment step, so other refund cases and cancellation are not checked by us.

A list of tests is not a safety check

Seeing a test in the table does not show that your water is safe to drink. A report covers only the parameters tested and the sample given. Ask the lab to explain the report. This guide gives no health or treatment advice.

Limits of this guide

  • We did not select any test, create an application, accept the terms, pay, give a sample or open a report.
  • We looked at one lab (Manjeri) in one category (Domestic). Other labs and categories can differ.
  • We did not check lab opening hours, sample-acceptance arrangements or turnaround time.
  • This is separate from KWA water-bill payment. See our KWA water bill guide for that.
