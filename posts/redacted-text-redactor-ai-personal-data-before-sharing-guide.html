[REDACTED] Text Redactor: hide names and places before sharing text

By AiCoN | Published 8 November 2026 | Sources checked 8 November 2026

Before pasting a support note, workshop message or draft into a chatbot, remove details that identify people. [REDACTED]'s Text Redactor finds likely names and places with a browser AI model and replaces them with labels. In our fictional English note, it hid a repeated name, Kochi and a demo email address. It is a review aid, not a guarantee of anonymity.

Quick facts

  • Use the Text Redactor, not a look-alike site.
  • The operator advertises it as free, without an account or usage limit. Our short test requested no login, card or payment.
  • The AI switch downloads a model labelled 29 MB. Pattern rules can find emails without AI; names and places need the AI option.
  • We checked: English text, AI detection, numbered replacement labels and copying the cleaned result.
  • We did not check: Malayalam, Hindi, PDFs, images, long documents, offline operation or security against re-identification.

Why use this before a chatbot?

The useful difference is the step before sharing. You can compare the original and cleaned text side by side, keep repeated people under the same numbered label, and copy only the cleaned version. The provider says detection runs on your device rather than sending your text away for synthesis or chat. That privacy statement is the provider's claim; we did not audit every network request or the code.

Our actual test

We wrote this fictional note, using a reserved example.com address rather than a real person's contact:

Fictional demo: Ananya Menon lives in Kochi. Email ananya.demo@example.com about the school workshop. Ananya Menon will collect the printed worksheets on Monday.

Before the model finished loading, the email alone was hidden. After AI became ready, the result showed four hidden spans: the name twice, the place and the email. We selected Numbered labels and used Copy. Reading back the clipboard gave:

Fictional demo: [NAME_1] lives in [LOCATION_1]. Email [EMAIL_1] about the school workshop. [NAME_1] will collect the printed worksheets on Monday.

How to use it

  1. Open the official Text Redactor and start with non-sensitive sample text.
  2. Choose the language and turn on AI if you need names, organisations or places detected. Wait until the model is ready.
  3. Paste the text in Original. Choose the categories to hide.
  4. Choose labels, numbered labels, blocks or [REDACTED]. Numbered labels helped preserve the repeated name in our test.
  5. Read both panes. Add missed words to Always hide these words. The page also lets you click a redaction to keep the original.
  6. Copy the cleaned text. Re-read it before sharing. The site offers a .txt download too; we used Copy, not the downloaded file.

What it can miss

A note can identify someone without their name. A rare job title, school, event, case number, story or combination of dates may be enough. Our sample left Monday unchanged. We did not check whether all dates, Indian IDs or phone-number formats are found. Remove unnecessary context yourself and do not use an automatic pass as approval to share confidential records.

The language selector we saw did not list Malayalam or Hindi. Do not assume an English success carries over to either language. We have not rated detection accuracy across different names, languages or document types.

Privacy and practical limits

The privacy page says files and text are processed in the browser, with the model fetched on first use. Its linked model page identifies the model used. We verified that AI loaded and processed our sample, but did not test with the network disconnected. First use needs a model download and may take time. The privacy policy also describes cookieless visit analytics and technical information such as IP addresses reaching hosting and model-download providers. Local content processing does not mean no network activity. The service says it is not for children under 13. Do not paste passwords, live credentials, private medical records or material you do not have permission to process into an unfamiliar tool.
