Ableton Learning Music: try making music in your browser

By AiCoN | Published 17 October 2026 | Sources checked 17 October 2026

Ableton's Learning Music and Ableton Learn teach music making in your browser, with nothing to install. Ableton said in 2017 it is free, and we found no current price.

What it is

Ableton is the company behind the music software Ableton Live. It runs two learning sites that teach music making in your browser: Learning Music and the newer Ableton Learn. Learning Music says: "In these lessons, you'll learn the basics of music making. No prior experience or equipment is required; you'll do everything right here in your browser." Ableton Learn says: "Learn the fundamentals of music making right in your browser." You click boxes and pads to play and combine small pieces of music. It is not an AI tool. We included it as a free way to try making music with no setup.

Is it free?

We could not confirm this on a current official page. Ableton's own blog post from 13 September 2017 says: "Learning Music is free, there's nothing to install, it works on any internet-connected device." That is an old statement. The current Ableton Learn pages we read do not mention a price, a login or a paid plan, and say there is nothing to download or install. So it looks free to try, but treat the 2017 post as the only direct statement we found. Ableton Live itself, the paid music software, is a different product that we did not check.

What you can do with it

  • Learning Music. The 2017 post says it covers beats, melodies, basslines, chords and song structure, and was available in English, German, Spanish, French, Japanese, Dutch and Italian at that time. The current site opens with a "Get started" lesson where you switch musical boxes on and off.
  • Ableton Learn. The current home page lists a Beats lesson on rhythm, tempo and drum sounds, a "Learn from artists" section with lessons from experienced musicians and producers, and says more lessons on basslines, melodies and chords are "coming soon".
  • Playground. Both sites have a Playground to try what you learned. Ableton Learn describes building your own rhythms and melodies with a selection of Ableton sounds.

What to watch out for

  • Ableton Learn is still growing. Its own page says some lessons are "coming soon".
  • The statement that it is free is old. See the answer above.
  • Beginner level. These are the basics. They are not a full music course.
  • Not tested. We did not play through the lessons.

Privacy and data

  • Ableton's Learning Synths site, from the same company, shows a cookie banner. It says clicking "Accept" means you consent to your data being processed in the United States and to cookies, and that if you are under 16 you should click "Close" to deny consent. We did not read the full privacy policy, and we did not check whether Learning Music or Ableton Learn show the same banner.
  • We did not check whether the Playground saves your music to an account or a server.

India

  • We found no region limit on the pages we read. No rupee price is involved. We did not test speed or access from India.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ableton Learn free?

We saw no price. A 2017 Ableton post says Learning Music is free. We did not find a current statement.

Do I need to install anything?

No, per Ableton Learn and Learning Music: it runs in the browser.

Do I need music experience?

No, per Learning Music: no prior experience or equipment is required.
