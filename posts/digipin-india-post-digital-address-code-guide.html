DIGITAL ADDRESS

DigiPIN: your address as a 10-character code - how to find yours

By AiCoN | Published 28 October 2026 | Sources checked 28 October 2026

Every roughly four-metre square of India now has a short digital code. DigiPIN, the Department of Posts' open-source addressing grid developed with IIT Hyderabad and ISRO's National Remote Sensing Centre, assigns a 10-character code like C4P-8K6-3M4M to each location, calculated from its latitude and longitude. Anyone can find the DigiPIN of their home or shop on India Post's free Know Your DigiPIN web app - we tested it on 28 October 2026.

Quick facts

  • What it is: per India Post's DigiPIN page, DigiPIN is an open-source national-level addressing grid - the foundation layer of a Digital Public Infrastructure for standardised, geo-coded addresses, built by the Department of Posts with IIT Hyderabad and NRSC (ISRO). The goal is simplified addressing for delivery of public and private services, or "Address as a Service".
  • How it works: the official technical document says the grid divides India's territory into uniform units of about 4 metres by 4 metres. Each unit gets a unique 10-digit alphanumeric code derived from its latitude and longitude, using a set of 16 symbols - the letters F, C, J, K, L, M, P, T and the digits 2 to 9.
  • How it is written: for people, the code is grouped with hyphens, like C4P-8K6-3M4M. The project's documentation says systems and APIs should always use the continuous 10 characters without hyphens or spaces.
  • It marks a place, not a person: a DigiPIN identifies a 4m square of land. It contains no name, house number or personal details - anyone can compute or look up the code for any coordinates, and it works offline as a location reference.
  • Open source: the reference implementation is public on GitHub (Apache-2.0 licence), so apps and services can build DigiPIN support without permission or fees.
  • Our test: on the Know Your DigiPIN app we entered the official sample code C4P-8K6-3M4M; the map jumped to that exact grid cell and labelled it (screenshot below). The app asks for your device's location to show your own DigiPIN - it states the location is used only to find DigiPINs for your area - and also offers search by location name or by a known code.

How to find your DigiPIN

  1. Open the Know Your DigiPIN app - dac.indiapost.gov.in/mydigipin - on your phone or computer.
  2. Tap I Consent and allow location access when the browser asks; the app centres the map on you and shows the DigiPIN for your spot. Stand at your gate for the most useful code.
  3. No location access? Use Search Location to look up a place, or Search DIGIPIN to check a code someone gave you - the search accepts the 16 allowed characters shown under the box.
  4. Note the code and share it with delivery services or anyone who needs to find the exact spot, alongside your normal address.

A DigiPIN complements your postal address rather than replacing it: the PIN code routes mail to a delivery office, while a DigiPIN pinpoints the last few metres. India Post also documents DHRUVA, a related layer of its digital address ecosystem, on the same page.
