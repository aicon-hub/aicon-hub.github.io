DPDP logo and tagline contest: closed status, prizes and original-work rules

By AiCoN | Published 7 October 2026 | Sources checked 7 October 2026

MyGov’s DPDP Act logo and tagline contest is closed. The official page gives 30 September 2026 at 11:45 pm IST as the end time and shows Submission Closed. This archive guide explains the former offer and its rules, not an invitation to apply now.

What was the contest for?

MeitY and MyGov invited citizens to create a visual identity for the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 awareness programme.

The theme included privacy, citizen rights over personal data, trust and responsible data processing. The proposed identity was intended for awareness material, events, handbooks and digital communication.

Current status

The official task page shows a start date of 3 September and an end date of 30 September 2026. It is marked closed when checked on 7 October.

No extension or reopened entry route was established in that page. Do not pay someone who claims they can place a late submission through a private route.

Logo prizes and tagline prizes are separate

The page lists one first logo prize of ₹1,00,000 and two second logo prizes of ₹50,000 each.

It separately lists one first tagline prize of ₹1,00,000 and one second tagline prize of ₹50,000. The original short post did not describe that separate tagline prize list. A listed prize is not a guaranteed payment to every entrant.

Tagline requirements

The tagline could have no more than eight words and needed to work in both English and Hindi. Submission requirements included Unicode text in both languages, not only text drawn into an image.

Unicode text is ordinary encoded text that can be copied and read by software. A picture of Hindi words does not substitute for the requested text field or file content.

Logo selection criteria

MyGov lists originality, clear communication, simplicity and scalability. It says the design must not resemble an existing government or commercial logo.

The identity should remain readable as a small website icon and a large conference backdrop, in colour and monochrome, and on light and dark backgrounds. The logo and tagline should work together.

Technical requirements

The page lists JPEG, PNG or PDF submissions, recommended up to four megabytes and at least 300 DPI. Winning participants would need to provide a vector source such as AI, EPS, CDR or SVG.

A vector file describes shapes rather than only pixels, making it easier to resize without losing sharpness. Renaming a PNG to SVG does not create an editable vector design.

What the submission needed to include

  • Colour and monochrome versions of the logo.
  • A combined logo and tagline arrangement.
  • English and Hindi tagline text.
  • Colour values and font details.
  • A design explanation of no more than one hundred and fifty words.

The creative could not carry the participant's name, watermark or identifying mark.

Original work and AI restrictions

The official rules say entries generated entirely by AI tools are disqualified. They also reject unlicensed copyrighted fonts, clip art or stock elements.

That is more precise than saying every use of a digital design tool is prohibited. The page does not settle every mixed human/AI workflow. A participant should seek the organizer's written clarification rather than assume an unclear workflow is allowed.

Terms limitation

The task text mentions Terms and Conditions, but the checked page did not supply a usable linked terms document. This guide therefore does not invent additional ownership, eligibility, judging or payment rules.

The visible prize and technical requirements are summarized here. They are not a replacement for the full terms an entrant needed before submission.

India and cost verdict

This was an Indian public contest, not a scholarship or a purchase. The visible task description invited citizens, but detailed participation conditions were not independently recovered.

No entry charge is stated in the checked task text. That is not a reason to send money to an intermediary, especially after closure. No registration or submission was made for this guide.

What an existing entrant can do

  1. Keep the original submission record and design source files.
  2. Preserve evidence of licences for any third-party elements.
  3. Check the official task and results announcements.
  4. Verify any prize message through official channels.
  5. Do not pay a prize-release fee to an unrelated contact.

No winner or payment was verified here. Closure and the number of entries do not prove a result has been announced.

What changed since the original post?

The deadline has passed and the page is closed. We add the separate tagline prizes and the precise entirely-AI-generated restriction. The guide is now an archive record, not a current apply-now offer.

Frequently asked questions

The checked official page says submissions are closed.

Are all entrants paid?

No. The page lists prizes for selected winners.

Were full terms recovered?

No. That limitation is kept explicit.
